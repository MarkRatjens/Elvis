**The Elvi Siege**

Dawn breaks over Sublime Point to reveal an extraordinary sight: the government forces who had surrounded the facility now find themselves surrounded. Streaming down from the mountain roads come hundreds of vehicles—white Lincoln Continentals with mismatched hubcaps, converted tour buses with Elvis murals, pickup trucks bristling with sequined occupants, and most dramatically, the tanks Eloise had gifted to the Tulsa McLeans, now painted with rhinestones and sporting enormous Elvis silhouettes.

The Elvi deployment is a masterpiece of organized chaos. They establish a perimeter around the government perimeter, creating concentric circles of siege. Elvi of every gender identity pour from vehicles carrying an eclectic arsenal: regulation military weapons alongside baseball bats, pool cues, golf clubs, and what appears to be an entire horn section's worth of brass instruments repurposed as clubs.

**Command Confusion**

In his mobile command unit, Gerry watches the tactical displays with grudging admiration. The Elvi have positioned themselves with surprising tactical awareness—controlling high ground, blocking escape routes, and establishing interlocking fields of fire. Their vehicles form impromptu barriers while their personnel dig in with the enthusiasm of people defending their Holy Momma.

"Sir," his communications officer reports, "we're receiving multiple hails from the Elvi forces. They're demanding the immediate release of Sarah Jacobson and Tony Supaporn."

From his harbour office, Dunhilton studies satellite feeds showing the full scope of the Elvi deployment. What was supposed to be a clean military operation has become a three-ring circus with live television coverage.

"Assessment?" he asks Gerry over their encrypted channel.

"Approximately four hundred combatants, mixed equipment, high morale but limited training," Gerry reports clinically. "They're enthusiastic but disorganized. More noise than threat."

"Engagement recommendation?"

Gerry pauses, considering. "None. They're a distraction, not a military force. Any action against them plays poorly on Alexander's live broadcast. Better to let them posture while we focus on the primary objective."

Dunhilton nods, watching an Elvine in a gold lamé jumpsuit set up what appears to be a rocket launcher made from repurposed fireworks. "Agreed. They're carnival performers with weapons. Let them surround us—it just makes better television when we complete the operation."

**The Elvi Position**

The High Elvis establishes his command post atop the largest tank, using a sequined megaphone to coordinate his forces. Around the perimeter, Elvi and Elvines dig foxholes while humming battle hymns about evolution and the Holy Momma. Some have painted their faces with glitter instead of camouflage, and several units appear to be using disco balls as tactical mirrors.

Despite the theatrical appearance, there's genuine military discipline beneath the sequins. The Tulsa McLeans anchor key positions with professional competence, while other units demonstrate surprising coordination for a force that theoretically learned warfare from watching old war movies.

"Great Teacher says no shooting unless they shoot first," the High Elvis announces through his megaphone. "We're here to rescue the Holy Momma, not start the next world war. But if they want a fight..." He gestures to the assembled force. "We'll give them one they'll never forget."

**Standoff**

What emerges is a surreal Mexican standoff: government forces besieging Sublime Point, Elvi forces besieging the government forces, and Alexander's television crew broadcasting the entire spectacle live to a fascinated nation. The quantum suppression fields continue humming, keeping Eloise's reality manipulation in check, while three separate command structures try to manage an increasingly complex situation.

Inside Sublime Point, Eloise watches the feeds with something approaching maternal pride. "Well," she tells Hayley, "I may have underestimated them."

Outside, the Elvi begin setting up what appears to be a full concert stage facing the government positions, complete with amplifiers and lights. If they're going to lay siege, they're apparently planning to do it with style.

The siege of Sublime Point has become the siege of the siege of Sublime Point, and nobody is quite sure who's in charge anymore.
