**Locations**: The Domain in Airborne territory; New Graceland in the Ghetto; remote locations of Dunhilton’s penthouse and congregation halls.

**Tone and Voice Sentence**: "Science tells us we are all stardust, and if stardust can become Elvis, imagine what else it can become!"

**Synopsis**:  
The Evolution Elvi enact their boldest plan yet: a unifying concert held at The Domain, deep within Airborne territory. Defying bureaucratic resistance and security challenges, the event ushers in an unprecedented gathering of Cloudborne and Airborne citizens under the banner of evolutionary unity. Half a million attendees bridge deeply embedded social divides, their shared humanity emerging through music, scientific enlightenment, and the collaborative joy of the occasion.

The concert, masterfully curated, fuses science-focused rock anthems with immersive visuals of cosmic and human evolution. The High Elvis delivers an unforgettable performance, donning an iconic jumpsuit blending Elvisian tradition with evolutionary symbolism. His heartfelt ballad, "Most Have Never Stirred," moves the audience to emotional introspection, highlighting the precious chance of existence shared by all. As the concert crescendos, attendees chant anthems of defiance against division, culminating in a climactic rejection of fundamentalism and societal stratification.

Observing from afar, CEO Piotr Dunhilton and Gerry Hardman sense the existential threat posed by the burgeoning movement. Talks of accelerating the TMinus-Virus plan circulate in hushed tones. Meanwhile, Pastor Rex Humble, leading his gospel-fervent congregation in the Ghetto, senses the tremor of revolutionary change, fearing the infectious allure of the Evolution Elvi’s message. 

The event concludes with the High Elvis boldly championing science, praising the "Holy Momma," the "One After the One," and "the Dawkins," rallying the audience against religion even as irony permeates the chant. The concert’s impact ripples far beyond The Domain, signaling that the battle for Elvi minds—and society’s future—is far from over.
