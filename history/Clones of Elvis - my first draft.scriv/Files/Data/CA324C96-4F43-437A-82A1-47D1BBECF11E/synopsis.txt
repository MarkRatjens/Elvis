**Alexander Infiltrates Sublime Point**

In his makeshift command center, Alexander Koalemos watches the breaking news feeds with theatrical intensity. Multiple helicopters circle Sublime Point, military vehicles block access roads, and news anchors speculate wildly about "terrorist threats" and "classified research facilities." But Alexander sees what others miss—this is his golden opportunity to create the ultimate finale for "Candid Criminal."

"Get me a full production crew," he barks to his assistant via encrypted satellite link. "ENG van, satellite truck, the works. We're going to Sublime Point."

"But sir, it's an active military operation—"

"Exactly. Breaking news. Government siege of my mother's facility. This is the story that transforms reality television into actual reality transformation." Alexander's eyes gleam with anticipation. "They can't refuse media access when I'm documenting the apocalypse itself."

Within twenty-four hours (becuase he’s been away), Alexander's convoy is racing toward the Blue Mountains: three vehicles carrying cameras, sound equipment, satellite uplinks, a truck full of props, and his most trusted crew. He rides in the lead van, already composing his narrative about exposing the Sages' reality-threatening conspiracy while rescuing Sarah and their unborn son.

**At the Perimeter**

Gerry receives the alert from his field commanders as Alexander's distinctive media vehicles approach the outer checkpoint. Through his field glasses, he can see the "Candid Criminal" logos, the satellite dishes, the unmistakable bustle of a news crew preparing for live broadcast.

"Tax me," he mutters, keying his radio. "All units, we have media inbound. Alexander Koalemos."

From his climate-controlled office, Dunhilton watches the same intel feeds on multiple screens. Alexander Koalemos—Eloise Shakti's theatrical son, the one media figure in Ulyssia narcissistic enough to turn any crisis into personal drama, yet influential enough to shape public opinion.

"This could work for us," Dunhilton says into his encrypted phone to his field commander. "Let them through."

"Sir?" Gerry's voice crackles with confusion over the secure channel. "Koalemos is going to broadcast everything live. The whole operation will be exposed."

"Think strategically, Gerry," Dunhilton replies smoothly. "Right now we have a standoff. Eloise is barricaded, we're suppressing her abilities, but we haven't actually breached the facility. Her son's presence changes everything."

Gerry processes this, his tactical mind working through the implications. "You want to use him against his own mother."

"I want to use him as leverage. Eloise won't risk her precious son on live television, even if she despises his theatrical nonsense. Koalemos gives us the perfect excuse to force entry—protecting media personnel from the 'dangerous weapons research.'" Dunhilton's smile is audible through the radio. "Besides, if things go badly, a dead celebrity makes excellent propaganda."

**The Convergence**

At the outer checkpoint, TRAMPs officers wave Alexander's convoy through after a brief "security inspection." The media vehicles roll past armored personnel carriers and mobile command units, Alexander's cameraman already filming through tinted windows.

"This is incredible access," Alexander announces into his handheld radio, broadcasting live. "Government forces have allowed 'Candid Criminal' through their perimeter at Sublime Point. What you're witnessing, dear viewers, is not just breaking news—it's the culmination of everything our investigation has uncovered about reality itself."

Gerry and Dunhilton, monitoring the live feed, exchange knowing looks across their separate command posts. Neither trusts the other, but both recognize opportunity when they see it. Alexander Koalemos, in his narcissistic obliviousness, has just handed them the perfect pretext for assault.

Inside Sublime Point, Eloise watches Alexander's convoy approach on her security monitors, her expression shifting from surprise to calculation to maternal exasperation. Hayley appears at her shoulder.

"Your son?" Hayley asks.

"Unfortunately." Eloise sighs. "Alexander has his own delusions of saving the day. The question is whether his theatrical incompetence will help us or destroy everything we've worked for."

The siege has just become a family drama broadcast live to the nation, with the fate of reality itself hanging in the balance of Alexander's ego and his mother's schemes.
