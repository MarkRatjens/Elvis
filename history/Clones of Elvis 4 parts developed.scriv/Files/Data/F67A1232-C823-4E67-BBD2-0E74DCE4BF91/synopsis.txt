
"Acceptable parameters for what?" Sarah's eyes narrowed dangerously.
"For... for understanding the full scope of Gyges technology," The Johnny hedged.
Kleoboulos and Solo exchanged glances. Chilo seemed to relax.
"Sarah," Chip said carefully, "I've seen enough Fridge Force files to recognize patterns. Remote location targeting, damage assessment protocols—these are hallmarks of weapons testing programs."
"You're overthinking this," The Johnny blustered. "Look at the communication breakthrough! We're on the verge of revolutionizing human understanding—"
"Human understanding of how to weaponize dimensional physics?" Sarah stood abruptly, ignoring the protest from her lower back. "How to trigger earthquakes in strategic locations? Destabilize reality anywhere on the planet?"
"Now you're being dramatic—"
"Am I?" She spun to face Chilo. "You've been pushing for faster testing, more risk-taking. Were you in on this weapons angle from the start?"
Chilo's habitual calm showed its first crack. "Progress requires courage. And sometimes... strategic thinking."
"Strategic thinking?" Sarah's laugh was hollow. "That's what we're calling it? Eloise isn't interested in interdimensional sock queries—she wants earthquake weapons."
"Mrs. Shakti understands the importance of maintaining strategic advantage," The Johnny said defensively. "In today's world, we must—"
"She's militarizing it." Sarah's statement hung in the air like a guillotine blade. "That's why she insisted I take charge. Not for my qualifications, but to keep me compliant—keep me in the family business while she turns cosmic communication into seismic warfare."
"The Gibson Desert test was promising," Chilo observed quietly. "Remote location, predictable magnitude, no casualties. Proof of concept for targeted geological intervention."
"Listen to yourself!" Sarah slammed her hand on the table. "You're talking about earthquake weapons like they're a research milestone!"
"All technology has dual-use potential," The Johnny said quickly. "Communications, medicine, defense—"
"Defense?" Sarah's voice cracked. "Or creating earthquakes on demand wherever Eloise wants them? What's next—triggering them under enemy cities?"
Solo spoke softly: "The power to communicate across dimensions inevitably becomes the power to destabilize them. The question becomes: who controls such forces?"
Sarah gathered her tablets, movements sharp with anger. "This meeting is adjourned. Johnny, prepare a complete safety and ethics review before any further testing. Chip, I want full documentation of anything in your Fridge Force experience that relates to seismic weaponization."
"Sarah, wait—" The Johnny attempted.
"No." She cut him off. "I've waited quite enough. While I've been marveling at interdimensional laundry concerns, I've apparently been perfecting earthquake weapons for Eloise."
As she swept from the room, only Chip caught the dangerous gleam in her eye—not just anger, but calculation. She wasn't just confronting Eloise; she was war-gaming scenarios where her unborn child might become a pawn in interdimensional conflicts.
"Well done," Kleoboulos muttered to Chip as the others filed out. "You've just accelerated a confrontation that could reshape more than just this project."
"Perhaps it needed accelerating," Chip replied, surprising himself with his boldness. "Better now than when the first weapon test goes live in populated areas."
Outside, Sarah paused at the elevator, her mind racing through contingencies. If Eloise wanted weapons, she'd get weapons—just not the ones she expected. Project Kebab had just become Project Chessboard, and Sarah intended to be several moves ahead.
