## A. The last line of the novel

- **The novel's last line is: "And that's when Sarah's waters broke."**
- Everything after it is **an extended epilogue** — a distinct movement, not a chapter of the novel proper.
- This confirms and fixes the previously-recorded Part 6 button. The timeline already carries the note that Part 6 conflict scenes recur on that line; the ruling here is that it is the **final** occurrence and the book's closing sentence. The birth itself still never happens on the page.

## B. The ascension

- **When Sarah's waters break, Little Clinton fulfils the Sages' prophecy and ascends as The One After the One.**
- The waters breaking *is* the fulfilment — the same beat closes the novel and triggers the epilogue.
- The ascendant is referred to in the author's ruling as **"the Dawkins"** and **"Dawkins."** This is **Little Clinton** — Sarah's son, "the Dawkins" of Elvi religion, whose recorded genetic father is Clinton Richard Dawkins via the Sages' foetal implanting. It is **not** the original Richard Dawkins. Keep the distinction explicit in any downstream note; the KB already carries "the Original Dawkins" as the Elvi's term for the historical man.

## C. The council

- Ascended, he finds himself in **a multi-universal council of fellow ascendants.**
- Author's characterisation, verbatim: they "behave exactly like small-time local politicians who unfortunately, have the power to decide the fate of universes in a similar way that a town council may approve or condemn municipal and private activity."
- The register is therefore **municipal bureaucracy at cosmic scale** — the book's established satirical method (cf. the Airborne cabinet's euphemism-comedy, the Elvi ministerial portfolios, PAPER's motto) carried up to the level of gods.

## D. The commission

- The council tells him **his universe was "consumed" in the process of making him ascendant.**
- **His first task in his ascendancy is to create a replacement universe.**
- He is **free to do as he pleases with it**, "though his work will be judged and used in evaluating the nature of his immortal existence."

## E. The refusal

- **He is appalled at becoming a God, and objects to the idea of Creating anything.**
- **The council informs him he has no choice, and cites plenty of bureaucratic ordinances to back it up.**

## F. The solution

- After **much internal conflict**, he devises a plan.
- He **"Creates" the required universe by exactly restoring the one that was consumed by his ascension — without him in it** — "and trusts that it will continue to evolve just as it was meant to before all this unfortunate mess occurred."
- So the delivered universe is the Clones universe restored intact and running on, minus the child who was born to end it. The god's single act of creation is a self-erasure.
- **The restored universe is not in any way aware of the restoration** (author 2026-07-30). Nobody in it knows it was consumed and remade; there is no in-world memory, record, or perceptible seam.
- **The one tell is what happened to Sarah's baby.** That is the single discrepancy the restoration leaves behind, and **it is an open question** — see §H.

## G. The prophecy stands

- **The Sages are correct in their prophecy** (author 2026-07-30).
- **It probably remains off page for this story.** The reconciliation between what the Sages foretold and what the ascendant delivers is not shown in this novel; the author's note is that **it could be explained in a sequel's prologue.**
- Consequence for drafting and for the KB: the Sages' recorded prophecy is **accurate**, and is to be treated as accurate. Do not draft, annotate, or infer a reading in which the Sages were mistaken, approximate, or subverted, and do not reconstruct the reconciliation here — it is deliberately withheld.

## H. NOT ruled — open questions, flagged not answered

*Recorded as open so a later pass does not quietly fill them in by inference.*

- **What happened to Sarah's baby** — the one trace the restoration leaves. Open, and **may never be answered if a sequel is never written.**
- What becomes of the ascendant after the commission is discharged.
- Whether the extended epilogue is entirely on the council's side of the frame. (Partly settled by §F: the restored universe has no awareness of any of it.)
- How the ascension relates mechanically to the [[../arc-tracker/gyges-disc|Gyges Disc]] machinery the novel has been arming for it — the five-disc pentagram (S167), the shared fractal/dream/foetal-neural pattern (S141/S142/S165), the Rotherhithe precedent. The ledger calls that convergence the most load-bearing thread in the book; the rulings above do not state how (or whether) the discs deliver the ascension.
- Where the extended epilogue sits relative to the existing Epilogue material about the Estonian Elvis.
