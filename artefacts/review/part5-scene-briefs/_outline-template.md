# Scene Outline — Template & How to Fill It

*The shape every close-of-Part-5 scene outline follows. Two blocks: **Synopsis** (the short summary for the Scrivener index card) and **Notes** (the working detail to write from). Plain English only — no craft jargon. Where the facts come from: [[../part5-closing-plan]] (which scene is next), [[../part5-close-decisions-log]] (the agreed decisions for the close), and [[../../working-principles]] §12 (what's canon vs plan vs the source of truth), §13 (dates), §14 (plain English).*

---

## How to fill this in — read first

- **The Synopsis is the plain summary for the index card** — what happens, start to finish, showing how the person starts and where they end up. Keep it plain; don't dress it up. Everything else goes in Notes.
- **Say where every claim comes from — this is the whole point.** Mark each one: **(canon)** or **(scene: "…")** if it's already on the page; **(log §…)** if it's a decision already agreed in the decisions-log or closing-plan; or **[mine]** if I made it up or it's my suggestion. Anything I can't mark (canon) or (log) gets **[mine]**, so nothing of mine sneaks in looking like fact. If I'm not sure whether it's the author's or mine, it goes in the questions, not the body.
- **Check before writing it down.** Get the scene's current name from the binder (names change; the binder is the truth). Check the important facts against the manuscript and the decisions-log, not my memory.
- **Get who-knows-what right — that's how the secrets stay secret.** One wrong "he knows" can give the ending away. The things that must never slip out: what Sarah really is (and that she's always known it), that Chanta doesn't know he's carrying or spreading the virus, what Little Clinton really is, and the virus/Hardy truth.
- **No year on the page.** Use days and months that show up in the story (18 March, Melbourne Cup Day, Sat 10 Nov); never a year. The real dates live in the author's Aeon file — don't work them out or guess them.
- **Leave room for the writing.** Sketch the pressure and what happens; don't over-specify the prose, and don't invent hooks the story doesn't already have.
- **Ask, don't guess.** Real choices go in the questions. Don't decide them for the author.
