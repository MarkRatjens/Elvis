## Slot

Opens the close on the Elvi side. Comes **just after "The High Elvis kidnaps Tony"** (`061A36DD`) — which is where Chanta gets his first sight of Rex Humble: the pulpit built from the Memphis Mafia Bar's own lectern, the sideburn-shearing bowl, Lonnie's dive remade as Humble's "New Graceland." *That* sighting is "Chanta's experience with Rex Humble" the author placed this scene after. **Nowhere near Sarah.** The song it produces is what the Rock Against Revival (Brief 03) stands on, so it must precede the concert. (Per [[../arc-tracker/humbles-schism]]; the "Pastor Rex Humbard" binder item `6FA1B4AF` is sermon research, not the beat.)

## POV & register

Chanta, close third. Deadpan and self-deprecating — the exact comic-songwriting texture already written for the top of the Johnny-enlists scene (the below-Layer pub, the schooner, "Evolution is…" forty-seven times, *flaccid/acid*, *selection/erection*, "I'm not writing hip hop"). **The comedy is the surface; the grief is the engine underneath.** It stays wry even as the real loss lands — never maudlin, never a sincere ballad about feelings. Chanta creates from raw feeling, not intellect, and half the joke is that the "Great Teacher" can't rhyme on command.

## Where & when

A below-Layer pub (the sandstone-black establishment, "there since the guy they named Bligh Street after," the barman who's "never been the same since the Rum Rebellion") — or the studio near Graceland, or on the spot outside the converted bar (see Open Questions). Time: early in the close, days after the festival is announced, **weeks before the 10 November concert**.
*Timeline anchor (for `timeline.md`): "weeks before Rock Against Revival."*

## On stage & what each knows

Chanta, essentially alone (a barman; perhaps a stray Elvi or two). Chanta knows: he's the conscripted Great Teacher (which he privately deprecates); he's been told to write the concert's anthem; he has just seen the **Memphis Mafia Bar & Grill — Lonnie's place, where he and Sarah took refuge, where Ajent Jones raided — converted by Rex Humble into a House of Worship.** He does **not** know what he is; does **not** know where Sarah is. The reader knows the concert needs a song; Chanta only knows he's blocked and furious.

## What this scene is for

- **Produce "Religious Minds,"** the anthem the whole Rock Against Revival rests on.
- **Establish that the song comes from grief and rage at the Holy Rollers — not from Sarah.** This is the decoupling: it retires the "reunion unblocks his writing" link (S146), and it's why keeping Chanta and Sarah apart all through Part 5 costs the concert nothing.
- **Pay the loss of the Memphis Mafia Bar** — a recurring, beloved location, now desecrated. The gospel arc's cost landing personally on Chanta.
- **Character turn:** blocked/dissipated → re-engaged. And it *shows* his creative law — the assigned, intellectual pro-evolution anthem is flaccid; the unassigned, furious elegy is number one.
- **Set up** Dunhilton's teeth (comic antagonist reaction, → the Pillow scene / war) and the song's global reach as the measure of the Elvi's cultural win.

## The beats

1. **The wrong song, failing.** Chanta grinds at the concert anthem the earnest way — the pro-*evolution* number he's been briefed to write. It's dead on the page: the ribonucleic-acid/*flaccid* dead end, "Evolution is…" written forty-seven times and never finished, *selection/erection*, "I'm not writing hip hop." He's writing *about* evolution because that's the assignment, and nothing lives.
2. **The desecration (already witnessed).** Under it all is what he saw in the kidnap scene: the Memphis Mafia Bar & Grill — Lonnie's grimy, beloved dive, his refuge with Sarah — remade as Humble's **"New Graceland."** Its own bar lectern is now the pulpit; the sideburn-shearing bowl; Holy Rollers where the regulars were; the room scrubbed bright and pious. He feels it as a personal wound: his place, his people, gutted and made devout. (This need not be a fresh visit — it can be the memory sitting under the failed writing.)
3. **The rage-block.** Now he can't write *about the loss* either — he's too angry at the Holy Rollers and their **religious minds** to get a word out. The anger jams him worse than the flaccid evolution lyric did. He circles it, drinks, fails again.
4. **The break — the rage is the song.** He stops trying to write the assigned pro-evolution anthem and writes **what he actually feels**: a furious elegy aimed straight at religious minds. "Religious Minds" pours out of him. The craft irony lands without being stated — he spent weeks on the wrong song; the right one was the one nobody assigned.
5. **The button.** *"Religious Minds went straight to number 1. All over the world. Dunhilton ground his teeth so hard he needed three emergency visits to his dentist."* The concert now has its spine.
