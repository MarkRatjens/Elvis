# Character & Medical Profile: Limb-Girdle Muscular Dystrophy (LGMD)
### Severe Early-Onset Subtype (LGMD R1 or LGMD R9)

This profile outlines a scientifically accurate, naturally occurring variant of muscular dystrophy that affects females. It features childhood progression and a fatal outcome in the late teens, tailored for narrative use.

---

## 1. Medical Fundamentals
* **Inheritance:** Autosomal Recessive. The character inherited one mutated gene from each parent. Neither parent shows symptoms, making the diagnosis an unexpected shock.
* **The Biological Cause:** Her body lacks the specific proteins (like Calpain-3 or FKRP) required to protect muscle cell membranes during normal contraction. Without these proteins, muscle cells tear, degrade, and are permanently replaced by scar tissue and fat.
* **Target Muscles:** Primarily the "girdle" muscles—the pelvic girdle (hips/thighs) and the shoulder girdle. 

---

## 2. Narrative Timeline & Progression

### Early Childhood (Ages 4–7): The First Signs
* **Symptoms:** Subtle clumsiness, a "waddling" gait, and difficulty keeping up with peers on the playground. 
* **The Gowers' Sign:** A classic medical milestone. When rising from the floor, she cannot stand up straight using her legs. Instead, she must "walk" her hands up her own shins and thighs to push her upper body erect.
* **Emotional Beat:** Frustration at sports or gym class, frequently tripping over "nothing."

### Middle Childhood (Ages 8–11): The Loss of Stamina
* **Symptoms:** Climbing stairs becomes exhausting and eventually impossible without heavy assistance. Toe-walking develops as her heel cords tighten and shorten.
* **Physical Changes:** Winging of the scapula (her shoulder blades stick out prominently like small wings because the anchoring muscles have wasted away).
* **Emotional Beat:** The transition from walking independently to using a manual wheelchair or mobility scooter for longer distances.

### Early Teens (Ages 12–14): Wheelchair Dependency
* **Symptoms:** Complete loss of the ability to walk. The muscles of the hips and upper legs are mostly gone. Weakness spreads heavily into the shoulders and upper arms, making it difficult to lift a heavy book, style her own hair, or raise her arms above her head.
* **Complications:** Scoliosis (curvature of the spine) develops rapidly because the core muscles can no longer hold her spine straight.
* **Emotional Beat:** Adapting to a power wheelchair. The loss of autonomy during a developmental period when her peers are gaining independence.

### Mid-to-Late Teens (Ages 15–19): The Terminal Phase
* **The Shift:** The disease shifts from a skeletal mobility issue to an internal organ crisis.
* **Respiratory Decline:** The diaphragm and intercostal (rib) muscles weaken. Her breathing becomes shallow. She experiences morning headaches, daytime fatigue, and a weak cough due to low oxygen levels. She requires a BiPAP machine (non-invasive ventilator) at night.
* **Cardiac Decline (Cardiomyopathy):** The heart is a muscle, and this variant attacks it. The heart muscle stretches, weakens, and fails to pump blood efficiently. 
* **The Fatal Outcome:** Death typically occurs quietly in her late teens, usually triggered by sudden cardiac arrhythmia, congestive heart failure, or a minor respiratory infection (like a common cold) that her weakened lungs cannot fight off.

---

## 3. Key Vocabulary for Dialogue & Narrative
* **Proximal Weakness:** Weakness closest to the center of the body (hips/shoulders), which is why she can still write or type perfectly even when she cannot lift her arms.
* **Contractures:** The permanent tightening of tendons, causing joints (like ankles or elbows) to become locked in a bent position.
* **Atrophy:** The visible wasting away of muscle tissue.
* **Cardiomyopathy:** Chronic disease of the heart muscle, the primary internal threat.

---

## 4. Scene Ideas for the Novel
1. **The Playground Benchmark (Age 6):** A scene where she tries to climb a ladder to a slide. Her legs give out halfway up, and her parents notice her using her hands to physically push her own knees up to the next step.
2. **The School Dance (Age 13):** A bittersweet moment highlighting her power wheelchair. While classmates dance standing up, her friends crowd around her, decorating her joystick controller or dancing with her by spinning her chair.
3. **The Nighttime Routine (Age 17):** A quiet, intimate scene showing the reality of advanced progression—the sound of her nocturnal ventilator humming, and a parent or caretaker needing to gently reposition her pillows because she lacks the strength to roll over in bed.
