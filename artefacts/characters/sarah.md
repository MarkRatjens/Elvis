# Sarah

## SEALED CANON — author-side only (never to appear or be implied in any review)

Sarah is **over two centuries old.** Her parents worked for **[[jamison-hardy|Jamison Hardy]]**, and that connection is how she received the **[[biology|TPlusVirus]] injection** as a child (age 7). The TPlus is the source of everything the surface only hints at — her "invincible" health, her never being ill, and her **real combat skill**, acquired across two centuries rather than arriving as a sudden mysterious gift. It is also the truth behind "can't get pregnant": she is **terrified of condemning a child to her own condition**, so she avoids it — self-protection, not infertility.

This is a **debt, not yet paid.** No reader and no other character knows it ([[kleo|Kleo]] knows and defers — "there are better times ahead for revealing such things"). It pays off when **Sarah confesses to [[chanta|Chanta]]**: the two centuries, the Hardy connection, the injection. Until then every review treats her biology strictly as planted mystery (see [[working-principles]] §5 — debts, not payoffs). Her combat skill, by contrast, surprises even her: on the page her "I don't know" is **genuine** ("it was the truth. She didn't know") — a TPlus capability surfacing unbidden, which she has not connected to her sealed nature. The reader's explanation comes only at her eventual confession to Chanta.

**Affect (standing trait):** Baseline detachment — a subtle, minimalist response to strong emotion and to any overstatement of the current crisis. This follows from the sealed longevity above: two centuries have shown her that most world-ending panics arrive smaller than advertised, so she underplays where others escalate. Genuine feeling surfaces as involuntary flickers (the smile at Tony, tears that catch her off guard) that she masters almost at once. **Toward [[chanta|Chanta]]:** she gives trust, and only trust — he is the one person she confesses her sealed nature to. Beyond that he draws the small involuntary warmth she shows anyone she's fond of (the broadcast-tears she fights down), and her constitution holds it there. His love for her is hopeless and one-sided; she meets it with trust and takes shelter in his devotion. See [[working-principles]] §4.

**Concern about Chanta (current state — seeds the close):** Sarah is already uneasy about [[chanta|Chanta]] before the viral crisis breaks. She noticed early on how he came through his [[legal-framework|PAPER]] interrogation without breaking, and she worries about the women he has been sleeping with and what he may be passing on. This unease is why she watches him, and it is the seed of the guarded hints she later gives [[kathy|Kathy]] — she steers without ever revealing what she herself is. **Sarah then Eloise arrives:** Slips out to the festival at thirty-four weeks pregnant, setting out as early as baby and bladder could agree — black bouffant wig, stick-on sideburns, poncho over a jumpsuit; dismisses the limo outside the old state parliament to walk in as one fan among thousands. The disguise fails theologically: her Holy Momma iconography has outrun her — posters of her in exactly that wig-and-sideburns costume hang on Elvi kids' bedroom walls — so [[walter|Walter]], a teenage fan, recognises her on sight and genuflects; she removes the disguise and lets her blonde hair show. Slips into an ironic regal register when Elvi theology demands it — commands the kneeling Walter up with a royal formula, greets the marshals with a single laconic word — but refuses the title face-to-face: "Don't call me that! Just 'Sarah', okay? Or Ms Jacobson, if you need to be offensively formal." — Jacobson, not Shakti, offered as her formal style. Adopts Walter for the day — protective, playfully regal: buys his silence with a promised introduction to the Great Teacher (Aunt Kathy thrown in), wields the aunts as carrot and stick (behave, or Aunt Hayley will jail you), accepts the marshals' escort to the throne on condition Walter comes too. Rushes the guarded portaloos before anyone can stop her; endures the ceremonial throne, sitting down in instalments; drinks juice; warns Kathy not to give the kid any patches. Bites even at Eloise: "What, do you think you're just one of the girls?" Saddened by Chanta's absence though she didn't expect him; avoids looking at his empty throne the way a tongue avoids a cracked tooth; learns from Kathy, reluctantly given up, that he's backstage — hospitality tent, among the musos, and rather more among the Elvisettes — draws her inferences and lets her imagination run. Sees Kathy and Hayley openly a couple; reads Hayley's nod as effusive by Hayley's standards. Recognises the Ermine Muller chair brand and guesses, with dread, who it's for before Eloise appears; relieved there's no dentist-assassin entourage, which she takes as hope of no point-proving executions; registers that Eloise — who found her without being told or invited — offers no reproach, no remark on security details, and hears calamity-grade threat in the mild "It would have been nice if you had invited me"; trades one barb and lets it lie. Exhausted, bladder-governed, vertebrae jangled — the body now sets the terms of her escapades. Past sundown the music re-associates her with her first Vegas Show — framed as possibly the last time she was happy — and the Graceland memory of weeping as one filament in a braiding of love that didn't end anywhere; she experiences both events in one present skin, a restored state of grace from before the Sages helped themselves to her future. Knows from her mis-spent nights in Elvi bars that music stopping mid-chorus means an ugly fight over a point of lyrical theology. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")
