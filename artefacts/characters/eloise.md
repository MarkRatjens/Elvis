# Eloise (Shakti)

**Voice:** Precise, clinical, minimal modifiers deployed for maximum effect. "She seldom used modifiers, especially adverbs, but when she did, she expected they'd be noted." Repeats "inanely ambitious" twice, escalating — [[alexander|Alexander]] never hears the qualifier. Surgical economy: "Mowing his lawn. In the Ghetto." Dry wit: "Absolutely, Alexander. Check your stocking come Christmas." Shapes "every word with care." Uses dramaturgical framing to reach Alexander on his own terms: "plot holes," "protagonist's objective," "arc of this story." (scene: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion") Four dialogue modes in the parlour — domination (sizing up/dismissing), interrogation (methodical dismantling), vulnerability ([[social-structure|Cloudborne]] backstory, schools, firebombing), proposition (succession plan). Uses "tax" profanity system: "May he rest without tax," "Men can be such taxers." Key line: "Celebrity will do that to a person." Calls tanks her "loan." Arms-dealer philosophy: "I never seek war, merely the profit from the threat of it." (scene: "Eloise proposes to [[sarah|Sarah]]")

**Decisions:** Calls Alexander immediately after seeing him on the broadcast. Warns him about PAPER gunships. Refuses to disable PAPER weapons — "Only if I fancy exile. Or a coup." Agrees to consider neutralising the [[the-elvi|Elvi]] tanks but explains the strategic consequence: PAPER then owns the field. Tells Alexander to stall until she arrives. (scene: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion") Enters parlour and immediately dominates — dismisses Alexander, negotiates with [[hayley|Hayley]], threatens Elvi with tank repossession. Interrogates Sarah about the baby's father. Reveals [[legal-framework|Candid Criminal]]'s true origin (Alexander wanted to target Airborne crimes; research team fed him fakes). Reveals her own Cloudborne origin, marriage to Lysander (Airborne), Stock Exchange injunction, pivot to arms dealing. Proposes that Sarah marry Alexander — succession play for her empire. (scene: "Eloise proposes to Sarah")

**Relationships:** Exasperated, protective mother to Alexander — "poor little Alexander," sent to all the best schools, all expelled him. Cannot quite bring herself to crush him despite clear-eyed assessment of his incompetence. Compares his obsession with Sarah to childhood pattern: loved his blue-tongue lizard Flynn Lizzy, tired of it, dissected it, couldn't reassemble it, sawed off extremities. Sold military hardware to PAPER — direct knowledge of their capabilities. Claims Alexander is "always being monitored." (scene: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion") Widow of Lysander (Airborne, "shrewd, if a bit dull"). Loaned tanks to the Elvi. Knows about John Stoat's claim to be involved with Sarah. Has surveillance on Sarah (photos from Blue Shade and Memphis Mafia bars with dates). (scene: "Eloise proposes to Sarah")

**Arc:** First major dialogue appearance. Arms dealer who sold PAPER their military hardware. Knows PAPER's gunship fleet and their operational patience ("they'll wait until you show your hand"). Heading to the siege location personally. Distinguishes police cruisers ("for quelling protests") from PAPER military hardware ("meant for real trouble"). (scene: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion") From arms-dealer power broker to someone who reveals genuine vulnerability about class, marriage, and motherhood — then pivots to a cold succession proposal. The vulnerability may or may not be performed. Cloudborne origin revealed; first Cloudborne founder to nearly go public — Stock Exchange filed injunction with Moral Compliance. Firebombed medical centres after Alexander was diagnosed as "not bright" with the implication being because his mother was Cloudborne. (scene: "Eloise proposes to Sarah")

**Halo Assaults Sarah:** Seizes mic from Alexander, conks him on the head with it — twice. Demands to know if he recognises Haloren Smythe. Shuts him down with "Good answer" when he denies it. Knows Halo's full name and identity — demonstrates she is already aware of his PAPER connection. Protective of the information: prevents Alexander from naming Halo on camera. (scene: "Halo Assaults Sarah")

**Eloise takes Sarah to Sublime Point:** Blocks Alexander from the elevator; redirects him with Candid Criminal Russia syndication. Expert animal handling — calms Chainsaw (devon rex) with detailed knowledge of breed neurology: "High intelligence, low tolerance for chaos. The key is becoming the most boring, predictable element in their environment." Separates Sarah, [[kathy|Kathy]], Hayley into townhouses at [[geography|Sublime Point]]. (scene: "Eloise takes Sarah to Sublime Point")

**Sarah and Eloise talk:** Domestic warmth mode: "Who's a good pussy cat? Who's a good Chainsaw?" Corporate death calculus: "Year-on-year we're down 3.4%." "The dead don't break non-compete clauses." New oath: "Holy accountants preserve us!" Metaphorical register: Three Sisters allegory for Sarah/Kathy/Hayley alliance. Breaking point: "Look Sarah, either work with me or throw yourself off this lookout right now. I'm tired of this." — **theatre, not genuine fury** (keeping people off guard is her method). Pre-arranged sashimi for Chainsaw (sourced before guests arrived). Offers Sarah a board seat, jobs for Kathy and Hayley. Plans to keep Alexander travelling internationally post-wedding via Candid Criminal syndication. Commits to keeping Sarah at Sublime Point until after birth. Expresses unqualified disgust at Alexander producing grandchildren: "no issue of my son shall inherit any... Anything." Identifies Hayley as "smitten with Kathy." Provides fresh hardware to Hayley rather than allowing retrieval of her own equipment — security/surveillance implication. (scene: "Sarah and Eloise talk")

**Hayley Interviews Chip Again:** Orchestrates the Gravity Well negotiation — attends briefly, plants explosive patch on [[chip|Chip]], introduces Hayley as her representative, then exits. Grandmotherly friendly smile; eyes glistening with compassion and sympathy (performed). "I so love tissue-thin raw meat, don't you?" Threatens with casual, surgical precision. Chip's resignation contracts already sent to Fridge Force lawyers before the meeting began. (scene: "Hayley Interviews Chip Again")

**Kathy fetches the Sages:** Surveillance mode — deploys "every surveillance trick in the book of espionage and contract bridge" to monitor Kathy. Allows Sarah's task (assigning Kathy to fetch the Sages) as "adjacent enough to the bell curve of statistically normal." Escalation: eyebrow raised at Kathy entering Ghetto; lips pursed at "Aunt Kathy" honorific; alarmed when semi-automatics appear (scrabbles for report on their provenance — NOT Shakti hardware); cancels all meetings to give complete attention. Closing beat: watches Sages arrive from sixty-third floor of a building "that existed for the sole purpose of not appearing to exist," reaches for fresh cup of tea. (scene: "Kathy fetches the Sages")

**Eloise rescues Sarah from the Elvi:** Accesses firmware backdoors on ALL military hardware (both PAPER and Elvi), issues theatre-wide shutdown — gunships land ignoring pilots, tanks freeze. References John Deere right-to-repair as legal precedent. Calls [[agent-jones|Agent Jones]] directly, threatens to detonate all hardware if he doesn't let them leave. Gives 10-minute deadline. Claims connections to people with "pay grades far above" Jones. Uses customer-service tone for threats. Invokes Warranties and Service and Proprietary Rights contract provisions as legal basis. Smashes an Elvi guitar on the limousine hood to break through a song blockade. Assesses Hayley, Kathy, and Sarah as potential employees — "bright, loyal, brutal." Thinks "Tax it" — done selling weapons. Deep down hopes she might rule the world one day. Calls Hayley, Kathy, Sarah "my dearest friends" to Jones. Refers to them internally as potential recruits whose arrival has improved her odds. (scene: "Eloise rescues Sarah from the Elvi")

**Alexander gets redirected / reassigned / VorpalTech Contingency:** Fabricated VorpalTech AG to warehouse Alexander in Liechtenstein. Pre-packed his bags, fuelled the jet, printed business cards — entire operation pre-arranged. Five-hook seduction pitch targeting Alexander's vanity (title), aesthetics (Sarah's pregnancy), greed (quantum lenses), paranoia (Dunhilton), ambition (CEO). Rare composure slips: "Oh, tax, now I'm blathering"; "She died a little inside" at calling Alexander not a complete idiot. Heinrich on retainer for "absurdist corporate espionage." Nightshade Protocol available (memory champagne or temporary kidnapping). Working from her smaller downtown office (not Shakti Tower). Engineered 36-hour flight delay. Hinted at a move against Dunhilton: "Soon I may have a little surprise for Dunhilton and his ilk." (scenes: "Alexander gets redirected," "Alexander gets reassigned," "The VorpalTech Contingency")

**Sarah then Eloise arrives:** Attends the mass Cloudborne festival in person, alone — no security entourage — in an understated business ensemble the narration prices above a small African nation's defence budget. Sends ahead a tailored young aide bearing an Ermine Muller office chair, squared to the stage with a surveyor's care; sits with perfect posture, eye contact with no one, watching the stage as if meditating over a ballet in which nothing will happen. Located [[sarah|Sarah]] without being told or invited and lets the arrival itself carry the message — no reproach for the escape, nothing about security details or notice, only "It would have been nice if you had invited me"; the narration notes she has said less and still threatened calamity for everyone in earshot. Minimal utterance, maximal menace — yet unexpected softness for [[walter|Walter]], the kid who hails her as "Granny" (no explanation given): "Hello, urchin," and a smile "for the kid." Self-ironising deflection when Sarah needles her about being one of the girls — asks whether she should ring her arms-dealer friends and arrange a night at the pub. Refers to Sarah's baby as her grandchild in a tone everyone on the platform hears as transactional. Accepts [[kathy|Kathy]]'s patch noncommittally and stows it in her handbag; lasts one band's set before pulling out her tablet and working mid-festival. Deflects Kathy's missile question to [[hayley|Hayley]] by publicly naming her "the Chief Security Officer here," then answers anyway: no missiles aimed at the stage, since the Bubble would frazzle them and cook everyone underneath — herself and her grandchild included. Hears Kathy's quiet aside to Sarah even over crowd noise. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**The Sages hijack the stage:** Seated beside Sarah in the Ermine Muller chair. Dances the opening number — "danced like she was just an arms dealer in a rock'n'roll band and sold it with ease"; competitors catching glimpses of her on the livestream end up more terrified of her than before. At the ditty's first performance, "the Ermine Muller chair held very still" — Eloise rendered wholly by chair metonymy. Steadies Sarah at the jumbotron reveal — puts her hand over Sarah's to stop its trembling: unprompted physical care in public, the gesture Sarah's interiority repays with the arms-dealing-mother-in-law concession. Narration age-marker: the Sages' beards are older than anyone in the audience, Eloise and one of her entourage included — Eloise (plus one unnamed member of her platform party) benchmarked as the audience's elders; per author ruling the VIP-platform party counts as her de facto entourage. (scene: "The Sages hijack the stage")

**"Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival]:** Customer-service register extended — threats *and forecasts* share it ("the mild customer-service register she kept for threats and forecasts alike"); modifier discipline restated in narration: "the one modifier she permitted herself she permitted because she meant it." Carried the telemetry tablet in her lap for two hours without looking at it; engages it only when the song presents a quantity — "She had heard the song the way she heard most things, which was as a quantity" — and runs the commercial arithmetic "cleanly and to the third decimal": hook "already installed in half a million skulls on a single play — the expensive part done for free"; every market takes it (the Ghetto for the tune, the Pale for the sneer, the Cloudborne for the novelty of an Elvi song that hated something); licensing, stream, merch. Forecast delivered "to the stage, to the night, to nobody": "Number one by Tuesday… Every market. Tax it." Then sets the tablet face-down and returns to genuinely enjoying the song, "which she was, enormously" — monetiser's instinct fully intact mid-festival, pricing the Elvi's cultural counter-offensive in real time while enjoying it. (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])
