**The mechanism (author-side reading, realised in the draft):** the block breaks here, and the mechanism is **self-belief, not a muse — this is crucial.** Chanta's writing talent is **innate**: his knack for naming the obvious thing everyone else steps around (the genuine innocent who blurts the truth). He needs neither Sarah nor Little Clinton — he only *believes* he does, and that false belief is the sole thing limiting him. He would have written "Religious Minds" cold sober in an empty room if he had only believed he could. On the drafted page, the psychotropic vision that seems to *deliver* the song is Chanta unconsciously **manufacturing the muse's presence to grant himself permission** — a placebo: alone up the engineering tower, off his face, he peers down toward the muse and gets nothing (the aura obscured, no inspiration), and the words flood only once the vision seats him beside Sarah — who doesn't turn, doesn't see him. The reader must catch the gap: he credits a muse who was never there, while the lyrics are plainly about *his* rage at the gutted Memphis Mafia Bar (Humble's "New Graceland") and *his* god's-eye look at the crowd — nothing to do with the baby. The rage, and the realisation that the Evolution Elvi worship Sarah and Little Clinton as religiously as the Holy Rollers worship God, are the **occasion and the subject**, not the cause; the unblocking is permission-via-belief. **Thematic rhyme with the ending:** the man who writes the anthem *against* religious minds has the most religious mind in the book — a congregation of one, kneeling to Sarah's belly — and never recognises the irony, exactly as the crowd never recognises "Praise the Dawkins." His block is a faith; his breakthrough is a placebo; "Religious Minds" is secretly about him. The draft keeps him and Sarah apart through the whole chapter — she never sees him; he flees toward the hospitality tent and skips his own premiere. (scenes: "Chanta's tower," "The Sages hijack the stage," "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival]; decisions-log §J, refined 2026-07-11)
