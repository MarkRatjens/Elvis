# The Elvi (collective)

**Voice:** Sing Elvis song titles as spontaneous commentary on events — "That's Someone You Never Forget" when [[sarah|Sarah]] is discussed; "Holy Momma! / Holy Momma!" when [[hayley|Hayley]] asks if Sarah is sick. Guards rock on the balls of their feet, flicking fingers to a beat before breaking into song. (scene: "Hayley questions [[kathy|Kathy]]") Liturgical responses: "The giver of the lyrics" (bowed heads, clasped hands), "Holy Momma" (rising reverence). Recognise Jones: "That's the narc who shot up the [[geography|Memphis Mafia Bar]]!" Deliberate "officer" provocation. Weaponised Elvis charm. (scene: "[[agent-jones|Agent Jones]] confronts Hayley") At Rock Against Revival: fight while "quoting mondegreens as if they were scripture"; deadpan hospitality at the gates — a Bubble Constable's hopeful "Drugs?" answered by an Elvine: "Cane sugar and peanut butter." (scene: "The three laws") Genuflection "in the Elvi way" — sunglasses removed, one knee down, head bowed, "Holy Momma" as reverent address; marshal courtesy-liturgy: "If you would like to come with us, your throne is prepared"; the frontman halts applause to announce her — "Friends, the Holy Momma has arrived!" — and the whole arena turns and cheers. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives") New call-and-response liturgy at the festival's closing address — attributed to the crowd at large, the Elvi a minority within it: Praise-the-Dawkins responses ("We're the lucky ones!"), the tax-populist chant "Tax the Colonel! Tax the Tom Parkers!", the civic chant (Clean the Domain!), and the law-echo (Sing the words of the Great Teacher!). (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])

**Relationships:** Revere [[chanta|Chanta]] — guards relax and nod to each other when Dr Logan vouches for him. Have stopped fighting among themselves. "Really starting to believe in themselves" (per Dr Logan). Dr Logan describes their relationship with Chanta's lyrics: "they lap them up." (scene: "Hayley questions Kathy") At the festival, factional self-segregation in the hospitality marquee — bands singing the Teacher's words eat on the left, bands intending to freelance eat on the right; violence never approaches Chanta's exempted corner ("a ring of Elvisettes and a general atmosphere of exemption"). Collective deference to The High Elvis: the Oath of the Warranty solemnises them, and his patient mic-tapping alone dissolves a general riot into self-consciousness. (scene: "The three laws") Holy Momma iconography saturates Elvi youth culture — posters of Sarah in full wig-and-sideburns costume on kids' bedroom walls; the familial honorific system extends to Aunt Kathy and Aunt Hayley among kids who have never met them; platform protocol enacts the theology — Holy Momma and Great Teacher enthroned, aunts on ordinary folding chairs. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**Decisions:** Guards at the mansion gates in camo ponchos and capes; let Dr Logan through without challenge; eye Hayley "like a pest they weren't quite allowed to poison." (scene: "Hayley goes to confront [[alexander|Alexander]]") Enforce lyric orthodoxy with violence at their own festival — Elvi charge the stage "at a dead sprint to argue using karate" whenever a performer deviates from the Great Teacher's words; some acts are punched on before even reaching the stage; combatants hang up their capes first ("there was justifiable violence and then there was dry-cleaning"). Volunteer as festival marshals who bless and direct foot traffic with karate hands; win over the Bubble Police at the gates with autographs, cocktail advice, and free tickets. (scene: "The three laws") Marshals locate the disguised Holy Momma in the crowd and escort her to the prepared throne; an apologetic young marshal "offered" Kathy and Hayley better seats, relocating the aunts from their picnic blanket to the platform; heavily guard the VIP portaloos beneath the engineering tower. Punch-ups over points of lyrical theology subside after The High Elvis lays down the law. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives") In the crowd at the hijacked headline set, those who know how to dance (mostly Elvi) jive, stroll and boogie-woogie to the opener. (scene: "The Sages hijack the stage")

**Holy Joint Venture:** Parachute insertion onto Accountancy Black lawns of St Hedges Cathedral — military-grade arrival at an Airborne wedding. All hair perfect post-descent. New liturgical gesture: cradle-arms (cradling newborn) during Holy Momma chant. Standing room only. "Not a dry eye behind their dark sunglasses." Declare Sarah "Holy Momma of the New Church of the One After the One." (scene: "Holy Joint Venture")

**Arc:** Songs broadcasting widely — "the biggest thing since Reggae pirate shanties." All lyrics written by Chanta. Cultural transformation underway: formerly fractious, now unified and self-confident under Chanta's creative influence. (scene: "Hayley questions Kathy") After Sarah's departure, maintain round-the-clock vigil over Chanta during his days-long grief. Internal factional dynamics: some ([[dr-john|Dr John]] / Dr Nick pairs) push medication; others block them with "thin, set lips and emphatic shakes of the head." Many wanted to wage war for [[eloise|Eloise]] Shakti — militant impulse transformed by Chanta's song "Let Your Momma Go" into collective grief and acceptance. "Old anger and urge for battle softened." Join the chorus, find guitars, learn the song as a crowd. Call Chanta "Teacher." An unnamed female Elvi initiates the breakthrough by asking him to sing what he feels. (scene: "Let Your Momma Go") Form a parliament ("newly formed Elvi Parliament of the Ghetto"). [[the-high-elvis|The High Elvis]] personally drives Chanta to new lodgings in a parliamentary Cadillac. Build and retrofit a luxury recording studio two blocks from Graceland for Chanta. Have "great-ish expectations" for new lyrics. (scene: "The Elvi relocate Tony") Band has laid down instrumental arrangements for 30+ tunes, waiting on Chanta for lyrics. Internal frustrations surfacing: TV-shooting, shoe arguments, near-brawls. Elvisette fan culture intensified — thousands camping outside studio with tents. Fan clubs ("Elvines") sending gifts including a python. Blue suede shoes are standard Elvi footwear. (scenes: "Tony (unintentionally) spreads Sarah's virus," "Recording is not going well") [[major|Major]] collective radicalisation: discover Dawkins' anti-religion writings and map them onto their own condition (Warranty prohibits religion; [[social-structure|Airborne]] feed them "Perfection of the Market"). Adopt Dawkins as "the One" / "the Original Dawkins" — prophetic guardian figure. Irony: become religiously fervent about denouncing religion ("Praise the One! Praise his atheism"). Discover evolution and natural selection; speculate Elvi genes could drive next evolutionary leap. Sing "Evolve Me Tender" (existing song from Part 2) in new Dawkins-as-prophet context. Mob Chanta, slam him into door beam (apparently kill him), keep vigil, celebrate his 4am revival: "He is risen!" (scene: "Tony & the Elvi research the Dawkins") Rock Against Revival: at their own festival they are a minority of the half-million crowd — "the genuine article — capes, real sideburns, the pressings themselves." Internal fighting resumes at scale: the orthodox-versus-freelance lyric split escalates from stage ambushes to marquee brawls to a general riot with almost every Elvis and Elvine in a punch-up (peaking when a Tickle Me series Elvine sings "is your arse filled with paint"), and the day ends with their conduct placed under The High Elvis's decreed three laws. (scene: "The three laws") The Great Teacher's lyrics propagate through the festival's bands — later acts know the words better, and the fighting stops as repertoire spreads. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives") Called as a section in the singalong during the Sages' hijack, they sing louder than their numbers might have promised, the warmth of the massed voices almost transcendent; given the Elvi-only rounds (the Sages' instruments silent), thousands of Elvi a cappella "made crystal of the air," notes of such purity the crowd is reduced to wonder — round after round, each quieter and sweeter, until the tune is "more hymn than ditty" and much of the crowd is in tears; they simplify to the repeated Holy Momma / unborn Son couplet and let the refrain expire, the jumbotron Holy Momma reveal riding over their final rounds. The consecration beat belongs to them: a minority of the half-million crowd whose massed unaccompanied singing converts the Sages' ditty into a hymn. (scene: "The Sages hijack the stage") At the closing address the festival crowd at large (the Elvi a minority within it) learns the "Religious Minds" refrain on one play without comprehending its accusation — bellowing "a line about not being able to go on the way they were going on, and going on exactly the way they were going on" — enjoying it enormously; the lawn privately runs the sand-grain sum and privately fails to make it come out, and ends the night with an enormous grateful noise meaning nothing beyond the noise. (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])

**Occupy Bent Street:** Voice — post-festival singing fully absorbs the mondegreen as doctrine: massed crowds converge on "We must go on as Elvi / With religious minds… Praise the Dawkins / He's the one!" after a factional contest among preferred misheard lines; an Elvine evangelist works the crowd with the monkey catechism ("Are we really descended from monkeys?" / "You are, but what am I?"), and Elvi testimony converges on "Even though I'm a descendant of a clone, no one has the right to think they are better or even different from me." Decisions — selective orthodoxy exceeding the three laws: out of respect for [[the-high-elvis|The High Elvis]] the crowd adheres to the first law (rendering the other two surplus), yet also refuses the Sages' ditty as unacceptable precisely because "it had been allowed by the High Elvis"; logistics arm of the occupation (coffee urns and barbecue trailers from the Ghetto, amplification jacked into a basement substation, tatami mats and karate lessons, bands admitting Airborne kids on "fairly simple guitar parts for only a song or two," four chords taught "with the patience they otherwise reserved for pet chimpanzees and drummers"); an Elvis scrawls "amphetamines" across the Airborne grocery list; only the Elvi eat the buttermilk biscuits in sawmill gravy; on stage the band improvises a song from [[chanta|Chanta]]'s cleaning pitch, and the drummer's thrown punch is settled by the other musos. Relationships — an unnamed Elvine claims provenance-authority (present in American Sound Studio the day the Great Teacher discovered the Words of the Dawkins), drawing continually refreshing mixed-class semicircles; the cross-class teaching relationship with the Airborne kids is formalised (karate, guitar, band slots). Arc — Elvi time codified in narration (4am "still last night"; rising before eleven "the single biggest risk of furniture and bathroom fixture shootings across the country"); the evolution doctrine now propagates peer-to-peer through open-air catechism rather than from the stage. (scene: "Occupy Bent Street")
