# Alexander (Shakti)

**Rank:** "Lieutenant" — self-styled, Eloise-sanctioned. Not an official rank in any Australian police force (running Aussie joke). "Never before seen in Ulyssian policing, mysteriously floating outside the command chain." Halo confirms: "Lieutenant is not even a real rank in the police force." The implication: Eloise helped sanction the self-styled rank through institutional leverage.

**Voice:** Two distinct registers — polished TV patter ("Good evening, my friends") and petulant internal monologue ("So unfair!"). Self-dramatising, theatrical even in private emotion ("Choirs of angels sang"). Uses Australian PM names as swear-word euphemisms: "Alfred Deakin," "Edmund Barton." Wheedling when outranked. (scene: "Alexander arrives at the bomb site") Deliberately ambiguous about [[legal-framework|Candid Criminal]] executions: "Of course they are. We don't have the budget to fake them." (scene: "Police interrupt Candid Criminal") In interrogation: inverts famous quotes with smug authority ("Reason is the last gambit of a scoundrel"). Uses endearments as dominance markers: "my old mate," "my dear friend," "my recidivating pal." Petulant when challenged about [[eloise|Eloise]]: "That's not fair, [[chanta|Chanta]]." (scene: "Alexander arrests Tony") Showrunner delusion applied to police work — frames investigations as entertainment production, using "script," "treatment," "MC" (main culprit), "producer," "production values," "executive producer." Key line: "Is a police officer really a police officer if they don't have a show?" (scene: "Alexander visits [[hayley|Hayley]]") Answers phone: "Public relations celebrity, Lieutenant Alexander Shakti speaking. How may you help me?" Cannot distinguish real cases from TV productions — asks "which show and episode?" (scene: "Hayley calls Alexander") On-camera register: performative generosity ("Of course, my good fellow"), self-interrupting monologues collapsing into narcissism ("Look at my face — this is a face you can trust"), expectation of applause after pauses. Breaks character when journalist departs from script: "Has this idiot even read the script?" Gets [[blake-mudrake|Blake Mudrake]]'s name wrong ("Well, Drake—"). Wheedling with Eloise: "Are you proud of me?", "You really do think it's ambitious, Mother?" Hears only what he wants — "inanely ambitious" registers as "ambitious." (scene: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion") Maintains showrunner framing under interrogation pressure: offers to write Hayley "a part," critiques her opening line as poor "character motivation," dismisses evidence as script notes from a rival "production team." Deflects systematically — every piece of evidence countered with a narrative pivot ("He stole them," "He's a cleaner, detective"). Patronising register: explains things "like explaining to a five-year-old." Panics when confronted with the unexpected — Dr Logan's arrival triggers "Someone give me a script!" Finds the concept of "the truth" unpleasant. (scene: "Hayley goes to confront Alexander") Entrance line at gunship standoff: "I think you've wandered onto my set." Plays the Mother card to neutralise Jones. T-shirt riff: Candies, "Candid for Conviction," "My Lawyer Voted Guilty," "I Heart The Wheel of Death." Name-mangles Hayley throughout: Lanyards, Hayley-kins, Hayley-puss, Holly-girl, Helly-Whiskers, Holy Lanyards. Calls her "Detective Inspector" (wrong rank — intentional garbling). Reframes Hayley's investigation as "story arcs" for Candid Criminal. Final beat: "I'm glad you got Chanta off the hook. He's really a very good friend" — first unperformed moment, potentially sincere. (scenes: "[[agent-jones|Agent Jones]] confronts Hayley," "Hayley fits most of the pieces together") Cowed by Eloise instantly — "But, mother..." → "Yes, mother." Sent to wait in the car. Inserts "Detective Inspector/Executive Producer" garble of Hayley's rank. (scene: "Eloise proposes to [[sarah|Sarah]]")

**Tell:** Blown pupils and colouring ears when discussing Sarah — physical obsession visible to Hayley. (scene: "Alexander visits Hayley")

**Physical motif (running joke):** Alexander bears a persistent resemblance to a koala — round face, beady eyes, snub nose, fuzzy edges, ears sprouting sideburns. Deployed across multiple scenes and POVs: Sarah sees him as "almost koala-cute, but just off enough to make Sarah want to disinfect her screen" (scene: "Alexander courts Sarah"); narrator describes "koala-beady" eyes, "koala-still reverie," "round face settling into cuddlier lines," "hands limp and as warm as a marsupial pouch" (scenes: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion," "Alexander arrests Tony"); [[halo|Halo]] calls him "you excuse of a marsupial" (scene: "Alexander lays siege to the mansion"). Reference doc "Koala Tic Options for Alexander" catalogues physical tics: nose rubbing like a koala snuffling, half-lidded koala eyes, death-grip clinging to objects, hunched stocky shoulders like a koala wedged in a branch. The resemblance is a deliberate running motif — not yet fully exploited but clues are seeded throughout.
