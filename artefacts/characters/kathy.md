**The blood pact with Sarah [PLAN — author-raised 2026-08-10, shape proposed; see [[../part6-master-plan]] §9]:** [[sarah|Sarah]]'s standing rule is that **nobody takes her blood** ([[sarah]] sealed canon — her mother's instruction, kept for two centuries). Kathy has already broken it: on the page, in "Kathy visits Sarah," she **takes Sarah's samples — blood, DNA, urine — while Sarah sleeps**, without asking, and diagnoses the pregnancy from the results. So **Kathy has held an impossible blood panel since Part 2 and has never once said so.** Her "invincible" assessment is not a figure of speech; it is a reading. What passed between the two women when Sarah found out is **the pact**, and it stays off the page until the immediate lead-up to the Humpening.

**What Kathy knows (author, 2026-08-10): the biology, and none of the history.** She is a competent physician with her own sequencer and real curiosity — give her Sarah's blood and she finds the virus in it, and she did. Corporate labs miss it because **labs run only the tests they are told to run** (which covers Sarah's two centuries of clean med-exams); Kathy is not a lab. So her "invincible" is a finding, not a compliment. What she has no route to, and **has never asked about**, is where it came from: not the two centuries, not [[../characters/sarah|Sarah]]'s mother, not the lab, not [[jamison-hardy|Hardy]], not the test case. Proposed pact terms: Kathy never takes blood again without asking, never sends anything to a lab she does not control, never writes it down, and **never asks where it came from**; Sarah keeps her, trusts her, and never mentions the patches. Two consequences worth having: Kathy's later concealment of the SARA readouts from [[eloise|Eloise]] is this habit continuing, not a new choice; and the drafted lineage scene already shows the pact working without explaining it — Kathy syringes [[chanta|Chanta]]'s arm cheerfully and takes an **invasive uterine sample** from Sarah where a blood draw would have been simpler, in the same scene, side by side. **The matched pair — she noticed [PLAN, author-decided 2026-08-10].** Kathy has held **both** samples since Part 2, and by then both carried the TPlus: Sarah's **blood, DNA and urine**, taken while she slept and sent out to a lab ("Kathy visits Sarah"); and **Chanta's blood**, drawn by syringe in the corridor and run **on Kathy's own sequencer** against the foetal sample ("Kathy determines the lineage of Sarah's baby" — the paternity test, after the Vegas Show, so he is already carrying it). **She saw what was in them, and she has said nothing for months.** Consequences that follow, and they are binding on the drafting:

- **"Invincible" is a finding, not a compliment** — she is reporting data.
- **"Ah! Well, what do you know?"** — her line on the paternity result — carries a second meaning available on a reread.
- **Her silence is owed to [[sarah|Sarah]], and to Sarah alone.** It is why Sarah trusts her, and why concealing the SARA readouts from [[eloise|Eloise]] costs her nothing: the same concealment continuing. What she holds about [[chanta|Chanta]] is not a confidence — it is a fact about a patient who has never been told and never asks. **He does not know he is carrying anything**, there is nothing between them to keep, and his trust in her rests on nothing more than her being the doctor.
- **In Part 6 she discovers the TMinus, not the TPlus.** She needs no deduction about the cure — only permission to act on what she already holds.
- **The price, and it must be paid on every page she appears on before the reveal:** her behaviour reads as ordinary competence throughout. The reader gets nothing to catch. Nothing in her manner may signal that she is holding anything.

The history stays sealed regardless — she knows the medicine, never where it came from, and never says a word of any of it aloud.

**Why this matters for the close:** it is what makes "Kathy Figures it Out" cheap and earned. When she blood-tests Chanta and finds **the same organism she has been sitting on since Part 2**, she deduces nothing — she has the mechanism whole in one moment. What she lacks is not information but **permission**, so Sarah's guarded hints are not hints to a blank slate but Sarah releasing her to act on what she already knows, in a room where nobody else can read a word of it. One quiet wrongness she carries: the only vector she can reasonably infer between Sarah and Chanta is sexual — she is wrong, never says so, and the reader who remembers the disc knows better than she does. **The seal holds, and holds harder:** Kathy knows the medicine and none of the history, and she never says any of it aloud — the one person who could explain has spent the whole book not explaining.
