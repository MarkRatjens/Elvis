# The High Elvis

**Arc:** Leads the Oath of the Warranty ceremony before the Vegas Show. Narrator flags future significance: "by story's end, would be known as The High Elvis." First appearance. (scene: "Vegas show")

**Voice ([[kathy|Kathy]] fetches the Sages):** Composed, patient, counselling register. "[[geography|Travelator]] Uncertainty, Aunt Kathy." Cordial but firm — corrects Kathy when she uses [[sarah|Sarah]]'s name (Tulsas snap indignant stares). Shifts weight foot to foot as composure develops hairline cracks during extended wait. Confesses tactical failure: "I should have ordered fried peanut butter and banana sandwiches." NOTE: Song-title speech is inauguration-specific ceremonial register, NOT his general speaking voice. (scene: "Kathy fetches the Sages")

**Holy Joint Venture:** Enters Airborne cathedral to cheers. Stands at right side when Bishop won't vacate throne. Waved Elvi chanting down with authority. Pinched the Bishop's seat when he vacated. No dialogue — composed, cheeky, patient. (scene: "Holy Joint Venture")

**Decisions (Kathy fetches the Sages):** Assembles [[tulsa-mclean|Tulsa]] Maclean combat squad on Kathy's arrival. Provides cavalcade of Cadillacs and Lincoln Continentals to Travelator station. Defers to Kathy as extension of Holy Momma's authority. (scene: "Kathy fetches the Sages")

**Close of Part 5 — Rock Against Revival (drafted):** the weary conscriptor becomes the triumphant strategist, now on the page: he mounts the Rock Against Revival festival as the [[the-elvi|Elvi]]'s cultural counter-offensive to the gospel contagion, decrees law for the Elvi mid-festival, extracts the counter-gospel song from [[chanta|Chanta]] under ultimatum, cedes the stage to the Sages without fuss — and takes it back mid-solo — and closes the festival himself with the One / One After the One doctrine and the premiere of "Religious Minds." Counselling register held throughout, never bombast — before half a million he deliberately does not use the Inauguration ceremonial voice. (scenes: "The Domain," "The three laws," "Chanta's tower," "The Sages hijack the stage," "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival]) **[PLANNED — remaining]:** the Humpening — built on Kathy's medicine and Sarah's guarded hints (mechanics: [[../world-rules/biology]]), turning a body-to-body virus into a sacrament, urging Elvi everywhere to "go forth and sanctify" — is not in the drafted festival scenes. (decisions-log §F; closing-plan §3)

**The Domain:** Sites the festival in the Domain — Airborne ceremonial ground enclosed under the western end of the [[geography|Eastern Suburbs Bubbles]], "not a place you got into by accident," the same ground where the Airborne hold their galas and executions. Entry secured by paperwork, not force: at his request Chanta's staff handled the bureaucratic cover, and of all the red tape he personally completed only the festival questionnaire — its altitude-deficiency-sickness item "the easiest NO box to tick that The High Elvis had ever encountered" (wry free-indirect interiority on bureaucracy). The festival is mounted by stealth: the permit declares "classical polka thrash metal," rock production gear travels manifested as orchestral equipment, and by the night before, a rock cathedral — "a black cliff of scaffold and cloth" — stands fully rigged without a single official inspection, an audience in the hundreds of thousands anticipated. "The permit said classical. What the city got was an uprising." (scene: "The Domain")

**The three laws:** Quells a festival-wide riot with no bombast — understated entrance ("at a stroll, in a suit that was doing none of the shouting"), steps around wrestling colleagues, taps a fallen microphone at four beats a second "as patient as a claims assessor" until the punch-up becomes self-conscious fist by fist. Recites the Oath of the Warranty (which "solemnised the Elvi while baffling everyone else"), then decrees three new laws for the Elvi — (1) sing the words of The Great Teacher; (2) do not punch up on anyone who does not sing the words of The Great Teacher; (3) sing the words of The Great Teacher — shown as three jumbotron slides he reads aloud as they cycle, justified as example-setting "for our younger fans." Frames moral instruction as legislation ("What we need is a little more legislation; we need to lay a little law down") and closes the decree with the catchphrase as statutory sign-off: "Thank you very much." Authority operates without enforcement — the mic-tapping alone shames an all-in stage riot into silence. The decree codifies Chanta's status into law (singing the Great Teacher's words becomes a legal obligation for Elvi), and he asks non-Elvi "friends and fans" to observe the laws voluntarily "as a mark of solidarity and respect." The day is live-streamed to Elvi across The Pale and the wider Ulyssian Meritocracy. (scene: "The three laws")

**Chanta's tower:** Festival register is flat, affectless calm — mild as a cricket commentator, long slow draughts of Pepsi, examining his fingernails mid-pitch; states his case the way a doctor names a dose and delivers even the ultimatum in the tone of a scorecard summary (Lillee caught Willey bowled Dilley, nineteen). "There would have been less fighting if there were more lyrics." Appears unannounced in the hospitality marquee, seated on an esky as if he'd been there all day, and waits for Chanta to look at him squarely before making the ask. Commissions exactly one song for his closing set — about the Dawkins, the Elvi as evolution's beautiful accident, everyone no better or worse than anyone else — then escalates when Chanta produces nothing but a denied mutter: "Mutter me one song in time for my closing set and I won't have you banned from the Ghetto," then instructs him to keep muttering. Pairs the honorific with the whip hand — addresses Chanta as "Great Teacher" while threatening exile; catches him singing under his breath and refuses the denial. Frames the festival as uniting the Elvi for a cause rather than wallowing in old rabbling ways; laments the day's fighting keeps showing the world the Elvi are worthy of being labelled an underclass. Knows the Elvi fought all afternoon on the festival grounds; knows Chanta sang something despite the denial. (scene: "Chanta's tower")

**The Sages hijack the stage:** Green-room beat: scans Chanta's pad, acknowledges it with a nod and a firm pat on the shoulder, sets [[the-high-fidelities|The High Fidelities]] to work on the tune — the wordless working trust with Chanta unchanged. Opens the headline set himself: four tight spots produce him on centre stage as if teleported, straight into a fast rockabilly tune. Not forewarned — the Sages are "unexpected company" to him; he learns of the hijack mid-set at his own microphone. Speaks twice: mic covered, to the uninvited Sages — "What the tax are you doing here?"; then, to the crowd, "The Three Sages of the Travelator, everyone!" with applause — bandleader pragmatism, announcing them because he figures on exciting the crowd rather than leaving a gaping hole near the end of the night. Challenged privately, he inclines his head — "not permission, exactly," but "the nod of a man moving an item down the running order to make room for a thing that had, regrettably, always been on it" — and folds the hijack into the bill. And he ends it: bandleader case law (he has seen more than a few solo hogs in his time, they prevalently cross the line, and once they do there is only one option) — primes his band in the dark of the stage, pulls [[kleo|Kleo]]'s plug mid-solo, and his brass and rhythm sections relaunch the program within a heartbeat. Accommodation now has a hard edge: he presents the Sages as an act on his running order and unplugs them the moment the solo costs him the crowd — showman first, host second. (scene: "The Sages hijack the stage")

**"Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival]:** Closes the festival himself. Deliberately not the Inauguration voice — "What came was smaller and closer, a man talking over a fence at dusk to one neighbour he happened to like": intimacy at scale, one neighbour extended to half a million. Opens by holding the lifted microphone in folded hands, keeping the silence until the crowd settles into it — the same de-escalation applied all afternoon to smaller crises ("a punch-up here, a stage invasion there, each talked gently down off its ledge with the patience of a man reading a gas meter"). Preaches the evolutionary doctrine kindly — the crowd came from "the one long blind accident," the accident's name is the One, the One After the One is coming — "the way a doctor names a dose, no thunder in it anywhere"; recites the Dawkins verse plainly, without music, "in the voice you would use for a child who had asked a real question," attributing rather than claiming ("a thing the Dawkins had left lying around for anyone who wanted it"). Publicly frames Sarah as the Holy Momma carrying the One After the One "out in front of her this very night," and sets the arrival as not tonight — states he asked, attributing the timing to the Holy Momma's confirmation (she "would deliver Him to them when the night was right for it and not one hour before"), presenting the two of them as coordinated and glossing it as the only honest booking in the history of the industry. Delivers an extended quoted closing address before the title-drop — a valedictory MC register folded into the counselling voice: preacher's "brothers and sisters" address; deadpan roast delivered as gratitude (thanks the merchandise tents for selling artisanal air to the Airborne at prices the air itself found embarrassing; thanks the Airborne guests for losing to their first Elvi cocktail, with directions to the hoses on the eastern fence; mock-thanks the Colonel for asking nothing of any man but his percentage — hospitality as roast, kept affectionate); Elvis-title mondegreens folded into plain speech ("always on my mind — all of you, individually"; a little less conversation and a little more action;); doctrine as compliment: "And you have been a beautiful audience, and I mean that in the strict scientific sense." Law and logistics as much as doctrine: orders the crowd to carry out its own rubbish — invoking the Elvi's Ghetto clean-up (shovelled, scrubbed, hosed) as shared precedent and refusing to leave somebody else's lawn looking like the good old days; bans exit violence, reprising the Second Law as the hard one that travels with an escort, and softening its application for wrong-words singers to pity, correction in writing, and being left standing. Retroactively absorbs the Sages' hijack into the festival as a public service announcement, teasing that the ukulele solo will live forever in defiance of everything the Dawkins taught about survival of the fittest. Introduces the premiere with a knowing warning aimed at his own congregation: "Well. The Great Teacher has a few thoughts about our minds." Announces the new song with a single unadorned title-drop — "Religious Minds" — naming the Great Teacher sole author "the way you hand over a wrapped parcel," calling no one up for a bow and offering no explanation (public honour paired with the author's guarded absence); knows the song is new and unperformed anywhere ("not once, not anywhere, not even for the band until this afternoon" — the band learned it that afternoon). Knows festival logistics cold — scalper prices (three months' wages), hose locations, the midnight last train; knows the full day's running order down to the morning three-pieces; knows the song's target before the lawn hears a bar of it. The counter-offensive lands as culture rather than revelation — the narration closes the festival as "not a revelation… a very good concert," ending on its own weightless terms, exit instructions given, the crowd singing the counter-gospel hook it learned on one play. Closes the set by setting the microphone back on its stand "as gently as he had taken it up." (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])
