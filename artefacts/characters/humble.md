> **Canon status (read first).** Two Humble beats are drafted: his introduction at the pulpit in **"The High Elvis kidnaps Tony"** (`061A36DD`), and a second appearance — mid-benediction in New Graceland as the counter-gospel song breaches his church — in **"Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival]** (close of Part 5; see Scene log). The intro paragraph **landed in the 2026-07-06 redraft** (name planted a beat earlier in the Cadillac; portrait recast to non-clone televangelist; punch bowl + Biblical Shears in) — see the [[../review/humble-introduction-and-placement|introduction & placement brief]] and the Scene log below. ## Core

The schism made flesh — and the only antagonist in the book who is promoted **by** the plot rather than driving it. Where [[dunhilton|Dunhilton]] holds a state, [[gerry|Gerry]] runs an intelligence service, and [[halo|Halo]] carries a spike, Humble arrives owning nothing but a vocation nobody had ordered and an eye for an opening, and the whole grotesque joke of him is that when the gospel schism split the Elvi, he recognised a market where his insignificance had been and filled it. He is human — a mere fan, not a clone, who loved the Elvi without a scrap of the equipment for it, sang them badly at open-mic nights, and swept the floor of a bar full of his betters; and who now, alone in a Ghetto full of Elvi, is the one they kneel to. He dresses the part — the plain suit, the styled-up hair, a fan's earnest costume modelled on the clean-cut early-series Elvi — and still lands, somehow, as a coiffed, pastel, broadcast-ready preacher on the very kind of channel the Elvi were raised never to watch. He is the one man in the room with no sideburns of his own to sacrifice, shearing everyone who has. That is the engine of his menace and his comedy at once: he preaches with the bottomless certainty of a man who cannot tell — and does not care to — being cured of insignificance from being touched by God. From the outside the two are identical, which is the whole grift: he may well believe every word, and it changes nothing. His threat is real — he sanctions the "rescue raids," he polices the flesh, and in the end he lets his own flock die of a preventable virus rather than break doctrine _(provisional)_ — but it is always the threat of a man running rings around no one but himself. He is **less** dangerous than Dunhilton (no army, no genocide budget, no reach past the Ghetto) and skewers something Dunhilton cannot: the [[social-structure|Airborne]] religion-of-the-market's mirror-twin — actual faith turned into a **product**, with a collection plate, a broadcast schedule, and a modesty code. The respectful-lampoon rule governs him absolutely (see [[../research/elvis-gospel-milieu|Gospel Milieu]]): **the target is Humble's apparatus, never the gospel itself.** The hymns are the real thing, genuinely beautiful, offering the Elvi the one homecoming the [[world-rules/social-structure|Grayling warranty]] denied them; Humble is merely the salesman who slapped his face on the doorway and took the takings. And he ends, alone in a neon-lit room that used to be a bar, dying of the exact natural selection he spent his ministry denouncing, wondering with his last breath whether Darwin had been onto something — which is the closest the book lets him come to being right, and it comes too late to help him.

**Model:** Lampoon of the American TV-evangelist apparatus and mentality — **every real referent kept strictly off-page** (the house method; cf. [[dunhilton|Dunhilton]]'s Dutton lampoon). The satire targets the machinery — the broadcast altar-call, the tearful conversion, the mail-in collection plate, the modesty-policing, the weaponised certainty — never a named person, and never sincere faith. Two off-page referents fuse into the one on-page name; **neither is ever named in the prose.**
- **Appearance & apparatus — Rex Humbard (OFF PAGE):** the pioneer who first built a *television* megachurch (the Cathedral of Tomorrow, Akron, Ohio), turning the broadcast altar-call and the mail-in collection plate into an industry. Humble's *look* is Humbard's — the lacquered helmet of hair, the pastel suit, the basted-for-broadcast sheen; the point of the recast is that he grooms toward the clean-cut early-series Elvi yet reads televangelist. Register also borrows the fire-and-brimstone line (Swaggart's tearful-downfall theatre, the slain-in-the-spirit altar-call), because the flavour is old-time Apostolic/Pentecostal, not smiling prosperity-gospel. **Do not name Humbard — or any real preacher — on the page.**
- **The name — Rex Dyson (OFF PAGE):** the Apostolic/Oneness-Pentecostal street preacher who, in Apostolic fan-lore, **baptised Elvis Presley (with Vernon and Gladys) "in Jesus' name" in Memphis** — and after whom Rex Humbard was reportedly named. So **Rex Humble = Rex (Dyson) + Humb(ard)**: the two off-page referents welded into one on-page joke. The deepest irony, never stated in the prose: Humble is named for the man who baptised the *original*, so every Elvi he "saves" is a clone dragged back to the very font the original himself knelt at. The contagion the Elvi dread as corruption is, at the root, **inheritance** — the return of the repressed the Grayling warranty tried to keep buried. Humble is the accident that reconnects the wire; he just took the credit. Deploy only as sub-textual pressure and structural rhyme.

**Voice:** Fire-and-brimstone showman-preacher; starts conversational, builds to a knee-dropping, mic-clutched, sweat-slick crescendo. Sin rendered as physical contaminant — "I speak of PRIDE," and the crowd shudders "as if pride was a physical contaminant." Elvi doctrine filtered through evangelical grammar until both are absurd: pride is "the sin that cast Lucifer from heaven"; the swivel of the hip "leads straight to the fires of HELL"; the "Seven Deadly Shimmies"; the forbidden fruit was "a fried peanut butter and banana sandwich" _(provisional)_. A Southern accent that thickened by half the day the infection took (he is human — no "programming"; the accent is affect, freshly cranked) _(provisional)_ — the on-demand tell is now on the page: he preaches over the intruding song "without breaking stride, the accent thickening as it always did the moment he needed it to" (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival]). Weaponised warmth: the intimacy of a man who wants your soul and your wallet with equal sincerity and sees no difference between them. Catchphrase _(provisional)_: "Get Right or Get Left." **Guardrail:** he is an opportunist who saw the schism and filled the market — but play the **ambiguity**, not a wink-to-camera cynic: from the outside (and perhaps from the inside) you cannot separate his conviction from his hustle, and he need not know the difference himself. That ambiguity is exactly why he can die faithful. Never let the lampoon land on the hymns.

**Props & bits (canon, 2026-07-05):**
- **The Biblical Shears** (DECIDED — Samson build). He wields, and grandly names, the "Biblical Shears," piously preferring them to the electric clipper: _"Nowhere in Scripture does the good Lord reach for a Number Three guard comb."_ The deflating truth — they are orange-handled **stationery scissors from Officejerks**; he blesses them weekly, wields them "like the jawbone of an ass," and has stopped thinking of them as a thing you might also use to open a bag of chips. (Build 1's "a sacrament that needs a power point is plugged into Babylon" line is on the shelf to graft in.) Developed prose in the [[../review/humble-introduction-and-placement|intro brief]].
- **The punch bowl** (DECIDED). Shorn sideburns fall into a **cut-glass punch bowl** on the pulpit — the last survivor of several identical bowls gifted to his **great-grandparents on the happy occasion of their shotgun wedding** — "half a bowl deep in dark, curling hair, like some obscene relic collection." **Not a decanter.** The heirloom doubles as a non-clone tell: clones have no great-grandparents.
- **The hair he doesn't have.** Humble has no sideburns of his own to give — his is the televangelist helmet. He shears what he was never issued.

**Relationships:**
- **[[chanta|Chanta]] / the Great Teacher:** Humble's doctrinal antithesis. In the kidnap scene Chanta watches him from the back of the room and can't decide whether to laugh, vomit, or start a fight he can't win — recognising, with self-hatred, how easy it would be to be caught in the rhythm. Humble's faction denounces the Evolutionaries' [[chanta|Great Teacher]] as chief apostate _(provisional)_. They likely never share a clean two-hander; their war is fought through crowds and contagion.
- **[[the-high-elvis|The High Elvis]] & the [[sages-prophecy|Sages]]:** watch his rise "with mounting horror,". He is the problem the [[../arc-tracker/the-gospel-contagion|Rock Against Revival festival]] exists to answer.
- **The Gospel Elvi / Holy Rollers:** his flock and his instrument. 
- **The Evolution Elvi:** The Humpening finally breaches even his own congregation (the walk-in-cooler discovery).

## Scene log

**Voice / Arc (The High Elvis kidnaps Tony — `061A36DD`, KEPT — recast LANDED in the 2026-07-06 redraft):** First on-page appearance. Glimpsed by [[chanta|Chanta]] from the back of the converted [[geography|Memphis Mafia Bar]], behind a makeshift pulpit "that three weeks ago had been the lectern where a maître d' turned away tourists in the wrong shoes." The non-clone recast is now **executed in the prose**: Humble "modelled himself on the clean-cut Elvi of the early series releases, with Elvi-inspired hair coaxed upward with as much faith as product," yet "still looked like a man beamed in to explain a payment plan, despite the pink suit, and teeth corrected for a camera that wasn't in the room yet." The narrator aside frames him as the third kind of preacher — the one who "recognised an opening in the market and filled it… honed a skill for explaining to camera what is wrong with you and how much the cure will cost." Preaches PRIDE as "the sin that cast Lucifer from heaven"; conducts the **Biblical Shears** ritual — orange-handled stationery scissors from Officejerks ("nowhere in His holy word did the good Lord reach for a Number Three guard comb") — a kneeling Elvine approaches, the crowd sings "with pelvis-free solemnity," Humble snips, the Elvine "flinched as if she'd been hit," and the hair falls into the **cut-glass punch bowl**, "the last survivor of several identical bowls gifted to his **great-grandparents** on the happy occasion of their shotgun wedding" (the non-clone tell). Raises his hands; the crowd rises and sways, "a sea of obedience." **Resolved 2026-07-06:** the name is now planted a beat earlier (High Elvis, in the Cadillac: "This guy calls himself Pastor Rex Humble"); the pre-recast "played a doctor" clone line and the "crystal decanter" are **gone**, replaced per the recast (superseded quotes live in git history only). **Keep** the good bones: the bar-lectern pulpit, the flinch, the sea of obedience.

**Voice / Arc / Knowledge ("Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival] — close of Part 5, DRAFTED):** Second on-page appearance. Three weeks ago consecrated the walk-in cooler beneath a Ghetto bar as **New Graceland** with "a coat of whitewash"; had the Biblical Shears blessed that morning. Mid-benediction over a dozen-strong evening congregation, "modestly appointed, beige and bonneted, hip-still" — the Modest Apparel Movement now on-page. Canon props recur: the cut-glass punch bowl "half a bowl deep in the dark curling harvest of shorn sideburns"; the Shears "orange-handled… indistinguishable to the untutored eye from the sort of scissors that will also open a bag of chips." No quoted dialogue — all narrated; sin-as-contaminant doctrine extended: "sin rendered as a thing you could catch off a floor if you left the door open." When the floor begins to sing, he lifts his voice over it and preaches on rather than stopping — "certain, sweating, saved," the accent thickening on demand. Misidentifies the song: takes it for "the Evolutionaries' racket carried on the night air, the apostate festival throwing its noise down" — he does not hear it as a song, and "He did not see." Knows of the festival and frames it as apostate, but is wholly unaware "that the floor of his church had begun, very softly, to learn the words": behind his back, in the second row, an [[the-elvi|Elvine]] in a beige sack mouths "we can't go on, we can't go on," soundless and careful, and a second pair of lips takes up "with religious minds," while he preaches on over them — the walk-in-cooler breach his provisional arc turns on has begun. (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])

## Provisional material — arc ore, NOT canon

From the junk sketches (`2885A359`, `60A087A7`, `18A4D182`, `7F9C84D7`). The author's steer for the arc; distribute and **dramatise** (the sketches narrate an essay — "In the third week … he emerged"; the book shows, it doesn't summarise). Do not treat as settled.

- **Origin:** NOT AN ELVIS CLONE, JUST SOMEONE WHO DRESSES LIKE ELVIS. Resist any temptation to have him sing, espaecially amny on Chanta's songs.
- **Rituals & doctrine:** the **Great Sideburn Sacrifice** (shorn hair collected in a wide cut-glass bowl — a repurposed bar vessel — as a "vessel of repentance"; **not** a decanter, per the recast); "Praise Be Panels" (conversion testimony); the **Seven Deadly Shimmies**; forbidden fruit as fried-peanut-butter-and-banana sandwich; the **Modest Apparel Movement** (Elvines in "shapeless beige sacks," bonnets, no hip movement).
- **Apparatus:** televised ministry **"Heavenly Hits with Pastor Humble"** — frenzied preaching → altar call → converts collapsing backward into "Salvation Spotters"; collection plates overflowing (rhinestones prised off jumpsuits, the odd Cloudborne coin); funds the building of **"New Graceland,"** a worship complex with stained glass of Elvis-as-Moses driving the moneychangers from the temple, mic-stand in hand; a gold-leaf, Elvis-themed Bible. *(author flag: reconcile New Graceland referent — decisions-log §G makes New Graceland the converted [[geography|Memphis Mafia Bar]] where he dies, not a separate complex.)*
- **Menace:** frames Evolutionaries as **apostates**, sanctions "rescue missions" — midnight raids by Holy Roller squads armed with Bibles and conversion hymns on portable players. Catchphrase "Get Right or Get Left." His face on prayer candles.
- **Death (The Gospel Truth — `60A087A7`):** discovers the [[../arc-tracker/the-gospel-contagion|Humpening]] virus immunity-ritual has reached his own congregation (the walk-in cooler); forbids Gospel Elvi to partake — "faith shall shield us." His flock starts dying of [[biology|TMinusVirus]]; most defect to the Evolutionaries to survive; Humble is among the last, propped in his pulpit, and dies "wondering if perhaps Darwin had been onto something after all" — the schism ended by "un"-natural selection. Play this the respectful-lampoon way: his sincerity earns him a thread of dignity even as the cosmic joke completes.
