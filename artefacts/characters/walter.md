# Walter

**Identity (author-confirmed 2026-08-07):** A Cloudborne kid, **not an Elvi** — a fan, enjoying a spurt of celebrity after sitting with the Holy Momma. The TMinusVirus targets the Elvi clone genotype, so Walter is not in its genetic path.

**Voice:** Teenage fan candour, Elvi-style reverence with zero filter, delivered flat: identifies the blimp in a literal deadpan run (it's a pig, big hat, smoking a cigar). Tells [[sarah|Sarah]] "Me and all my friends have posters on our bedroom walls—of you, dressed exactly like that," then appends, looking at her belly, that the posters aren't pregnant. Greets the arms dealer with "Hello, Granny." (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**Decisions:** Recognises Sarah through her festival disguise and genuflects in the [[the-elvi|Elvi]] way — sunglasses off, on one knee, head bowed; ignores her hissed order to get up but rises for the regal formula. Trades his silence on the Holy Momma sighting for a promised introduction to [[chanta|the Great Teacher]], and asks for Aunt [[kathy|Kathy]] too, because she's hot. Takes the Great Teacher's empty throne when Kathy offers it and sits like he owns it; when the frontman announces the Holy Momma, stands and salutes, taking the crowd's adulation like a politician. Later stands at the platform rail waving at friends. Asks Kathy what the floating pig is, after first checking it's moored to the tower. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**Relationships:** Reveres Sarah as Holy Momma; visibly disappointed when she sheds the disguise — he wants the icon. Crush on Aunt Kathy: the selfie she offers turns his day up to eleven, until he catches Kathy and [[hayley|Hayley]] holding hands. Addresses [[eloise|Eloise]] familiarly as "Granny" (no explanation given); she smiles for him. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**Arc:** First appearance. A young Elvis fan among thousands who ends the day enthroned between the Holy Momma and Aunt Kathy. Has never been the top of anything — glows at Kathy's "top of the class." The narration tags him as almost certainly destined to be an entertainer. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**Knowledge:** Knows Sarah's poster iconography intimately (hair yes, sideburns pass, jumpsuit not so much). Knows the aunts by honorific unprompted. Learns from Kathy that the blimp is Colonel Tom Porker and the hat a Stetson. Holds a standing promise of an introduction to the Great Teacher. (scene: "Sarah then Eloise arrives")

**First appearance:** "Sarah then Eloise arrives" (Rock Against Revival, close of Part 5).

**PLANNED — Occupy Bent Street (author, 2026-08-07):** Walter is to be seeded into the Bent Street occupation scenes, **but not into the virus deployment scene**, which stays short, cold and clinical from [[gerry|Gerry]]'s window. The point is to give the massacre a face the reader already loves without warming up the prose that delivers it — Walter is spent *before* the gas, so that the clinical scene detonates against something already in place rather than having to carry the emotion itself. The load-bearing constraint holds: what he is given must be a *forward-looking* want (a future he is visibly planning), because the horror is the cancellation of the plan, not the death of a bystander.

**CHOSEN STAGING (author, 2026-08-07): Mayor of the Yurt → the Frogurt Yurt.** The plaza society, inventing itself, elects Walter mayor of the yurt — and he takes it *seriously*: converts his mayoralty into a frozen yoghurt outlet ("every festival needs an ice-cream parlour") and names it the **Frogurt Yurt**. The kid the narration tagged as destined to be an entertainer gets his first constituency, and his forward-looking want is his new career/enterprise. His fate in the release: **he survives** — as a Cloudborne fan he is not a genotype target (see Identity above); what the gas cancels is not his body but his world — the constituency and clientele that elected him and queued at his counter.

**Injection method (author, 2026-08-07):** Minimal-touch seeding, no fuss.
- The mayoralty lands **at the point the crowd is big enough — that's the point** (the office exists because the crowd has grown to the size that invents offices). **No election scene**: state it as fact, in as bald a declarative as can be constructed.
- The Frogurt Yurt gets **two simple statements at most**.
- Thereafter Walter is injected in **single sentences anywhere** in the occupation scenes — especially **offering frogurt to the riot cops** ("The Monday conversation," alongside the fried-sandwich and fool's-gold offerings). The release scene stays untouched per standing decree.
- Author's beat, near-verbatim: the frogurt turned out to be **the best way Walter had ever met girls** — he almost forgot [[kathy|Kathy]] — and **he loved to watch them eat the stuff**.
