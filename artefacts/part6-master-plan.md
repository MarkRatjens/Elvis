## 1. The shape

Twenty-two chapters. Two cutting rules, both the author's:

- **Chapters pair pieces from different arcs**, as the earlier parts do ("Gee,
  I Bruise," "Blue Shade Blues") — one chapter, two threads.
- **Fine grain on the backstory**: the three backstory documents are not
  chapters. They decompose into fragments distributed through the part **in
  binder order — Hardy, then The medicine, then A Big Crush**: H1–H4 (chapters
  2–5), M1–M3 (chapters 7–10), BC1–BC3 (chapters 12–16). The slices are
  proposed seams in existing drafted prose — regroup them at will.

Placement logic: Hardy is paired against present-day scenes that rhyme with
him (the cure announced against the cure spreading; Hayley touching his
misfiled papers; the heist's "nobody ever knew"). The medicine runs well
before the confession. A Big Crush lands the favour before it is called in
chapter 21.

## 5. What infecting Chanta does to Sarah

*The author's question, restated (2026-08-10): Sarah has the virus, the disc
absorbed it, Chanta contracted it. **Either that terrifies her, or there is a
mitigating factor that eases her reaction** — and at present she seems not to
care much. She does already twig that he is looking preternaturally well.*

**The answer is both, in sequence, and it is organised by one recognition.**

**Why it should terrify her.** Her defining terror is already sealed canon:
she is **"terrified of condemning a child to her own condition,"** which is
the truth behind "can't get pregnant" — self-protection, not infertility. The
disc took the virus out of her and put it into Chanta. She has therefore done
the exact thing two centuries of care were designed to prevent, to the one
person she trusts. That has to land as horror the first time she understands
it, or her restraint everywhere else is worthless.

**Where it already is on the page.** She perceives his miraculous healing as
**"betrayal layered on betrayal"** — which the reader takes for jealousy (he
is well; she is ill, pregnant, abandoned) and which is actually recognition.
And her established unease — *she worries about the women he has been sleeping
with and what he may be passing on* — is the displacement: she cannot face
what she did to him, so she frets about what he does to others. **Neither beat
needs rewriting.** They need Part 6 beats that let them be re-read.

**The three mitigations, and the order to show them.**

1. **It saved his life.** He was dying of the Stickett Inn bombing — burns,
   lungs — when the dose landed and healed him overnight. Whatever she did to
   him, the alternative was a funeral.
2. **The dose is diluted.** He is not Sarah-grade ([[world-rules/biology]]
   gradient). He heals fast; he still suffers — hangovers, torture, ordinary
   misery. He is not sentenced to two centuries. She learns this by watching
   him, and so does the reader; it is the difference between having infected
   someone and having condemned them.
3. **She was drugged and unconscious.** Chilo's potion; she was asleep. It was
   done *through* her, not by her — though she would never accept that as
   absolution, and shouldn't be written as if she does.

**The recognition that organises all of it: she has become her mother.**
Janine Anstey stole a virus, gave it to a dying child, could never explain,
and imposed the rules — *tell no one, never let anybody take your blood.*
Sarah has now given the same virus to a dying man, cannot explain, and
silently enforces the same rules on his behalf without telling him they exist.
Her apparent calm is not indifference and not even composure: **it is the
exact behaviour she was raised inside, and she has had two centuries of
practice at it.** The reader has been watching her perform her mother's
silence without knowing that is what it was.

**What this buys, structurally:**

- It is why "The medicine" fragments must land **before** the confession. The
  reader needs the mother's silence in hand to recognise the daughter's.
- It gives the confession (ch 15) its engine. Sarah is not disclosing that she
  is old. She is **apologising** — *I did this to you, and I did it the way it
  was done to me* — which is a scene, where a disclosure is only information.
- It gives the reunion (ch 6) its real motive. "Against her better judgment
  she cannot refuse him" is not weakness: **she owes him**, and cannot say for
  what.
- It makes M2's rules (tell no one, never let anybody take your blood) pay
  twice — once as a mother's instruction, once as something Sarah is quietly
  doing for Chanta.

**Minimum beats needed in Part 6:** one moment where the terror is visible and
unexplained (the reunion is the natural place — he is in front of her,
preternaturally well, asking to stay); the gradient made visible at least once
so the reader can see he is *not* becoming her; and the confession carrying
the apology rather than the announcement.

**Footnote — why nobody else is dosed.** Worth having settled, though it was
not the question: the disc **attacked** Sarah, was pulled off, and latched onto
Chanta's hand seconds later ([[continuity/object-locations]]). That is a
needle-stick, not a contaminated surface, and a virus needing a living host
does not persist on inert alien metal. Discs are otherwise inert unless spun.
The same disc has sat in Johnny's lab at Sublime Point ever since, handled
bare-handed by every technician on the project, all of them conspicuously
ordinary — which Kathy can use as a negative control when she is reasoning
her way to Chanta at the inauguration, and which forty Elvi carting sixty
discs about after the heist restate for free.

## 6. The disc rig — variable disc counts

The author's own answer is the good one and should become canon: **a circular
track on which any number of harnesses can be mounted**, each harness able to
spin its own disc, the array calibrated to focus the energy waves the discs
emit. This solves what the drafted lab chain fudges — it makes 1, 3 and 5
discs the same machine at different mountings rather than three different
rigs; it gives Chilo something physical to do when he adds discs without
authorisation; and it gives Chip a concrete engineering objection (the track
was calibrated for a smaller array). Note that the drafted scenes call the
five-disc arrangement a "pentagram," which the Sages would like and the
engineers would not: on a track it is five harnesses at seventy-two degrees,
and the gap between those two descriptions is available as both comedy and
characterisation.

## 7. Chapter 22 — the last chapter, two scenes

**Author's design (2026-08-10), recorded verbatim in substance:** chapter 22
is two scenes; it must be last because **it is the reveal that every other
"And that's when Sarah's waters broke" has been pointing at**. It is the final
piece of drama, in which **Eloise appears to break Sarah**, and sequentially in
time it is the last thing that happens. It goes **straight after Sarah loses
control of the control room**, and she seeks Eloise out about it.

- **22a — The Doors Burst Open** [to write]. The doors burst open on Chilo's
  hand at the controls (chapter 20's cliffhanger discharges here); every
  strand converges on the chamber; and **Sarah loses the room** — the array
  climbing with nobody at it she can overrule.
- **22b — Sarah confronts Eloise** [to write]. Having lost the room, she goes
  to the one person who owns the building and could still stop it. Eloise
  appears to break her. Then the final sentence.

**Consequences this settles.** The placement problem is resolved by the
author's own design: the drafted terrace two-hander (takeover, board leverage,
seven-day suspension) **moves back to chapter 13** as *Sarah takes the
project*, where its clock still works and where it sets up chapter 14's
revelation that the concession cost Eloise nothing. The name *Sarah confronts
Eloise* travels forward to the new scene at 22b. Chapter 21 is left to Tony
and Alexander.

**What 22b has to carry, and the one caution.** "Appears to break" is doing a
great deal of work: it must be the worst blow in the book, landed by the
character who has spent the whole part being right, and it must be
**immediately overtaken** — the waters break, the prophecy fulfils, and
Eloise's victory is obsolete within a sentence of being won. The caution is
that a blow strong enough to break Sarah is a blow the reader must believe
Eloise would land, and §4's Eloise — who values competence, negotiates values
later, and is satisfied her legacy is in good hands — has just spent chapter
14 being generous. The break therefore wants to come from being *right* rather
than cruel: the thing she says is true, Sarah knows it is true, and there is no
answer to it. What exactly it is, is the author's to choose.

**What parallelism does NOT cost** (correcting an earlier note of mine that

where a scene's business plainly runs past the instant, the elastic form is
available: **"And n minutes later, Sarah's waters broke."** So every drafted
ending in the set stands as written, with the line added; nothing needs
truncating to accommodate the device.

**One exclusion, and the reason.** *"Chilo makes his move"* is a nemesis
face-off and would seem to belong — but its ending chains directly into

not on it, and it keeps its own cliffhanger ending. The arrivals and the siege
build-up are excluded for the same reason at a larger scale: they are hours
and days earlier.

## 9. The blood rule and Kathy — the pact

**The problem, as raised:** Sarah's rule is that nobody takes her blood; but
Kathy is her doctor, and takes blood and DNA. Something has to give. The
author's own instinct — *a pact between them, never fully revealed to the
reader until the immediate lead-up to the Humpening, which makes Kathy
figuring it out much easier* — is the right answer, and **canon has already
set it up better than a designed version could.**

**What is already on the page.** In "Kathy visits Sarah," Kathy **takes
Sarah's samples — blood, DNA and urine — while Sarah sleeps**, without asking,
and diagnoses the pregnancy from the results. So the rule has already been
broken, off Sarah's consent, in Part 2. And there is a second sample: in
"Kathy determines the lineage of Sarah's baby" she syringes **Chanta's blood**
for the paternity comparison — after the Vegas Show, so he is already carrying
the TPlus. **Kathy has therefore held a matched pair since Part 2, and she
noticed** (author, 2026-08-10). She has said nothing for months. Full
consequences in [[characters/kathy]]. Which means:

- **Kathy has held an impossible blood panel for months and has never said so.**
  Her "invincible" is not a figure of speech; it is a reading.
- The breach is the crisis that forces the pact. Sarah finds out that the one
  absolute rule of her life has been broken by the person she was starting to
  trust, and the results are in a stranger's machine.

**Kathy knows there is a virus. This is the correction that makes the whole
thing work** (author, 2026-08-10). She is a competent physician with her own
sequencer and an incurable curiosity; give her Sarah's blood and she finds
what is in it. Corporate labs miss it because **labs run only the tests they
are told to run** — which covers two centuries of med-exams, including the
"perfect health, pregnancy negative" panel in Part 1 — but Kathy is not a lab.
So the line to draw is not *whether* she knows, but *what*:

- **Kathy knows the biology.** There is a virus in Sarah's blood; it is why
  she does not get ill; "invincible" is a finding, not a compliment.
- **Kathy knows none of the history.** Not the two centuries, not the mother,
  not the lab, not Hardy, not that Sarah was a test case. There is no route
  from a blood panel to any of it, and **she has never asked** — which is the
  pact.

**Proposed terms [mine], withheld from the reader until the Humpening
lead-up:** Kathy never takes blood again without asking, never sends anything
to a lab she does not control, never writes it down — and **never asks where
it came from**; Sarah keeps her, trusts her, and never mentions the patches.
Not blackmail (the patches are common knowledge) but the founding act of
Kathy's loyalty: a doctor deciding her patient's privacy outranks her
curiosity, and later her employer.

**What the reader gets in the meantime** — surface only, unexplained: a
formality that arrives between them and never quite leaves; Kathy
conspicuously *asking* before every procedure afterwards; and, best of all, a
clue already sitting in the drafted lineage scene, where Kathy syringes
Chanta's arm cheerfully and takes an **invasive uterine sample** from Sarah in
the same scene, where a blood draw would have been simpler. Nobody remarks on
it. It is right there.

**What it buys the close.** Exactly what the author predicted, and more once
Kathy is allowed to have identified the virus. When she blood-tests Chanta and
finds **the same organism she has been carrying in a locked file since Part
2**, she does not have to deduce anything: she has the mechanism whole in a
single moment. What she lacks is not information but **permission**. So
Sarah's guarded hints at the inauguration are not hints to a blank slate —
they are Sarah releasing Kathy to act on what she already knows, which is a
far better scene than a briefing, and it plays in front of a room without a
word of it being legible to anyone else.

Two further gains: it retro-explains Kathy concealing the SARA readouts from
Eloise (not a new choice — the same concealment continuing), and it gives
Kathy one quiet wrongness to carry, since the only vector she can reasonably
infer between Sarah and Chanta is sexual. She is a doctor; she deals in
vectors, not narratives; she is wrong, she never says so, and the reader who
remembers the disc knows better than she does.

**Where the seal now sits.** Not "Kathy knows there is something" but **Kathy
knows the medicine and none of the history** — and, crucially, she never says
any of it aloud. The reader still learns nothing until Sarah's confession,
because the one person who could explain has spent the whole book not
explaining. That is a stronger seal than ignorance, and it has precedent:
[[characters/kleo]] already knows and defers.

Recorded in [[characters/kathy]] and [[characters/sarah]].

## 12. Binder mechanics

Three layers of work, not two:

- **Moves and new folders** — I can do this with Scrivener **closed** (the
  hard rule: structural edits while the app runs are silently overwritten by
  autosave). Re-parenting scenes cannot touch prose; git checkpoints before
  and after; verified by re-parsing. You can equally do it by drag-and-drop
  from the running order.
- **Fragmenting the backstory documents** (H1–H4, M1–M3, BC1–BC3) — content
  surgery, not just structure. Ten fragments out of three documents is
  **seven splits**: three in "How Hardy killed the cure," two in "The
  medicine," two in "A Big Crush." Scrivener's own **Documents ▸ Split** is the
  safest tool and keeps snapshots and metadata sane; recommended you make
  these splits in-app at the proposed seams (or move the seams as you see
  fit). I can do them programmatically instead, but Split is genuinely better.
  **The seams are described by content, not yet by an actual cut point** — the
  running order says what each fragment holds, not the line it starts on.
- **Recommended combination**: you make the seven splits; then quit; I do the
  full restructure (twenty-two folders, all moves, retitles, label/status
  settings) in one closed-app pass; you reopen and read. Search lags until
  Scrivener reindexes — expected, not damage.
