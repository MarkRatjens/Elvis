# Mining the 1st Draft

*What is in `Clones of Elvis 1st Draft.docx` that is not in the WIP, and what of
it is worth recovering. Advisory only — nothing has been changed in the bundle.
Read alongside [[working-principles]] §12: everything here is a **suggestion**,
not canon and not plan.*

Line numbers below refer to a plain-text conversion of the docx, so they are
approximate signposts, not citations. Each item also names the draft's own
section heading, which is the reliable way to find it.

---

## 1. What the first draft actually is

Twenty-eight chapters, roughly 100,000 words, breaking off mid-sentence at
"The night of long archives". It runs from a geopolitical prologue to the
Humpening and the poisoning of the Airborne water supply. It is recognisably
the same book: Sarah, Tony (as "Cracker"), Shakti, Eloise, Halo, Hayley, Kathy,
Johnny, Agent Jones, Kleo, the Sages, the Elvi, the Gyges Disc, the Dawkins
child, the two viruses. Most of the furniture survived into the WIP.

Three things separate it from the WIP, and they matter for what is worth
lifting:

**The world is thinner.** The Primary Trade Dominion is a satirical sketch —
levels 1 to 7, a CEO, a Commissioner. The WIP's Airborne/Cloudborne/Layer
arrangement, the Meritocracy, PAPER and TRAMPS, Latter Day Capitalism, are all
better. Nothing in the draft's political architecture should come back whole.

**The narration is coarser and often does the cruelty itself.** Much of the
draft's nastiness — particularly about women, and about the poor — sits in the
narrator's voice rather than in a character's head, so there is no one for the
reader to hold responsible. The WIP has almost entirely fixed this by pushing
the same material into Halo, Jones and Chanta, where it belongs. When lifting
anything from the draft, this is the one thing to check every time: **whose
head is it in?**

**The plot is looser but braver.** The draft throws away large ideas at speed —
a fake execution, an economists' panel buried alive, a hidden control room with
a mummified billionaire in it — and doesn't linger on any of them. Several of
these are better than what replaced them, precisely because nothing replaced
them: the WIP simply dropped them.

## 2. The five worth taking seriously

Ranked by how much they'd add and how cleanly they'd fit.

### 2.1 Why Hardy buried the labs — the economists' panel

*Draft: ch22 "Majority Report", ~L3217–3305; also "Viral Lockdown" ~L3309.*

This is the single most valuable thing in the draft, and it fills a hole that
is currently open on the page.

In the WIP, Gerry tells Dunhilton that Hardy "shut everything down — buried
the labs under more concrete than several Ulyssia Harbours could hold."
Dunhilton asks, "He didn't let the staff out?" Gerry says, "Apparently not."
And that is where it rests. Nobody knows why. (scene: "Gerry proposes a
solution to the Elvi problem")

The draft answers it. Hardy learns his own lab has produced a cure for every
human disease. He locks a room of anonymous young accountants and economists
away and gives them seven hours to model the economy of a world without
sickness — the deadline set, privately, by how long it takes to mix the
concrete. What they come back with is one sentence: *the miracle drug must be
destroyed, all research must be destroyed, and all related research staff must
be eliminated.* He pours the concrete on his own friend Professor Hermann, and
reflects that he has just saved the entire world and no one will ever know —
"the curse of the true hero."

Why it is worth having:

- It is the book's thesis rendered as one piece of office admin. A cure is a
  catastrophic supply-side event; capitalism's immune system destroys it; the
  man who does it is congratulated by history and by himself.
- It gives Hardy a moral shape without putting him on the page — he stays
  the referenced-only figure the WIP has settled on ([[characters/jamison-hardy]]).
- It converts "Apparently not" from a shrug into a horror the reader carries
  while Dunhilton and Gerry never do.

The draft's staging around it — the glass dome, the radiation cook-up of the
panel, the murdered security manager, Hardy present and reflecting — is
scaffolding and does not survive the retrofit. What survives is the brief, the
one line they wrote, and the fact that the room was never opened again.

It cannot be a Hardy chapter — he never appears. It is told by the narrator,
direct to the reader, and no character in the book ever learns it. Room, form,
length and placement are worked through in §6.

### 2.2 The origin of Sarah's dose

*Draft: ch27 "Fight at the end of the Tunnel", ~L5369–5395; completed in
"Anti-virus Virus", ~L5495–5529.*

**Author rulings, 2026-08-08 — these supersede the draft's version:**

- Sarah **started life poorly**, and that is what moved her mother to **steal
  the virus from work**.
- **There is a scene** for Sarah being given the virus by her mother.
- Sarah's mother then **disappeared — she was in Hardy's seal-in.** That gives
  Sarah **two reasons to loathe Hardy**: he owned the project that made her what
  she is, and he buried her mother alive in it.
- Sarah has had two centuries and **has worked out what happened.** She is not
  ignorant of it; what she chooses to tell anyone is a separate matter.

This closes the question the KB had flagged as unresolved (why the mother did
it) with love and theft rather than accident, and it is better than the draft's
answer in every respect.

What the draft has, for reference only: Sarah is twelve; her mother's boyfriend
is a virologist who has been sending her mother presents and never Sarah; a
package arrives; Sarah opens it because she wants a present, reads only *drink
the vial*, and does. Three doses. Twenty years later her mother and sister are
dead in the viral cleansing, and Hermann says it flatly — *"You killed her, you
know that don't you?"* — with Sarah answering, *"Self-loathing is difficult to
deflect and even more difficult to endure."*

The draft's version makes Sarah complicit in her mother's death. The author's
makes her bereaved and owed, which is a stronger footing for the loathing. The
one thing worth carrying across is the register of that exchange — flat,
unconsoling, and much later than the event.

**Consequence worth noting:** if her mother is among the entombed, then Gerry's
"Dr Hamish Hermann and Doctor Anstey, entombed alive. **There were more**"
already contains her. The reader can hold that while Gerry logs a number and
moves on.

Caution: sealed material. It belongs where the author places it, not earlier.

### 2.3 Chanta's debt over Alexander — and the crime scene run by remote control

*Draft: ch8 "A Big Crush" ~L769–783 and "Shakti does something right"
~L899–911; ch12 "CSI by Remote Control" ~L1539–1583; cashed in at ch27
~L5731–5743.*

**Author, 2026-08-08: there is a scene for the early days of Alexander and
Chanta.** So this is placement, not invention.

The original's material, in order:

- **The Big Crush** (~L769–783). Cracker on clean-up six years earlier:
  thousands crushed standing and melded upright by rigor mortis, too slow to
  prise apart, so a front-end loader. He ducks off to be sick and finds the new
  police recruit already in the best spot. *"Is that real beef?"* — *"You can
  have it. I don't think I'll need it."* Then: *"They hadn't actually become
  friends that night, Shakti lacked the ability to connect, but they had become
  allies. The shared distress over the fatalities of 'The Big Crush' was the
  foundation of their alliance, also the starting point of their respective
  journeys to claustrophobia."*
- **The debt** (~L899–911). Handed an investigation he cannot begin to conduct,
  Alexander begs; Tony extracts a promise — *"In the future I will ask for
  something and you will deliver, no questions asked."* Alexander agrees.
- **The pay-off** (~L1539–1583). Alexander walks a homicide scene wearing camera
  glasses and an earpiece while a level-two cleaner feeds him every line. He asks
  salient questions, allocates resources judiciously, jokes with the forensic
  team, and his own department is astonished at his sudden competence. He also
  says "fuck" for the first time in his life because Tony told him to.
- **Cashing it** (~L5731–5743). Tony reminds Alexander of the debt in front of
  Sarah and takes her away from her own dinner with him.

Why it is worth having: the WIP's Alexander/Chanta relationship is entirely
one-directional — interrogation, coercion, the sneer through the limo window.
The KB already marks "I'm glad you got Chanta off the hook. He's really a very
good friend" as his first unperformed moment; this is what that line is the
residue of. An unredeemed promise gives Chanta a card to play that isn't a
punch.

Caution: it cannot soften Alexander into a decent man. The comedy is that he
thinks he has a friend.

### 2.4 Agent Jones turned, and made to serve

*Draft: ch23 "An afternoon at Cracker's" ~L3441–3585; then the tunnel warning,
the ear-biting at the testing facility (~L5869–5887), the sabotage of the
samples (~L5885–5893), and "Great Protector" (~L5947).*

**Author, 2026-08-08: Jones is not part of the economists'-panel material.**
This entry stands on its own and does not connect to §2.1.

In the draft, Hayley and Sarah catch Jones interrogating Tony and break him —
Sarah puts him through a wall; Hayley makes him confess to murdering Emil's wife
and daughters; then, instead of shooting him, Hayley tells him he now works for
her. He spends the rest of the draft as a double agent: slowing the drill team,
delivering the blueprints, and finally whispering into the ear of a captive
Elvis — *"I am with you, Elvis… I am going to bite your ear off now"* — so he
can retrieve the virus sample hidden behind it in front of an audience of
colleagues who applaud the theatre.

Why it is worth having: the WIP's Jones is the most humiliated character in the
book — Memphis Mafia Bar, Truth Central, outranked by Eloise, out-manoeuvred at
the siege — and his line as logged is defeats without a turn. A Jones who has
been made to serve the people who beat him, is quietly good at it, and is never
thanked in any way that counts, is funnier and sadder. The ear-biting is exactly
the book's register: an atrocity performed as a favour, applauded by the wrong
room.

Caution: the WIP has him as non-human product stock, which changes what
"turning" means — a unit reassigned rather than a man converted. That may be an
asset or a complication; worth deciding before anything is drafted.

### 2.5 Two set pieces the WIP has nothing equivalent to

**The fake execution.** *Draft: ch24 "A Shot out of the Blue", ~L3805–3873.*
Cornered by ten officers with an open bounty on Tony's head, Sarah shoots him
herself, point blank, and claims the promotion. A sergeant puts two more in him
for good measure. She stops him shooting Tony in the head — "I want him
identifiable" — takes the pulse, calls the meat wagon, bribes the driver, and
rides to the morgue with the body. Kathy certifies life extinct. Tony is now
officially dead. He asks how she knew he'd survive: *"I did not know, but did not
have much choice."* — *"You bet my life?"* — *"Yes and you won."*

The best single scene in the draft, and the WIP has no equivalent: the only place
where Sarah's absolute nerve and her willingness to gamble another person's life
sit in the same gesture. It solves the same problem the WIP solves with a tank
through a wall, but with a decision instead of an army. Not reusable as-is
(Chanta's rescue is drafted and better placed), but the manoeuvre — Sarah saving
someone by appearing to destroy them, betting on the virus without admitting she
knows about it — is available.

**The wedding-cake stabbing.** *Draft: ch26 "Slightly Red Wedding",
~L5031–5065.* Alexander, holding the cake knife in his cutting hand, raises cake
to his mouth, stabs himself in the eye, flails, and opens Sarah's carotid. Kathy
operates on a first-aid-room table; the artery has already reattached itself.
Eloise arrives to confirm the death so she can start seizing the assets, and
finds Sarah standing up. *"My dear, I thought you to be dead."* — *"No such luck,
Eloise."*

The WIP does reveal-by-violence differently (Halo in the service elevator), so
this isn't needed. But **Eloise arriving to inventory a corpse** is a character
note of extraordinary economy and is not in her file. That, at least, is worth
stealing.

## 3. Worth considering, second tier

**The Elvi rebuild the Ghetto.** *Draft: ch21 "The High Elvis" ~L3123–3141;
ch25 ~L4437–4457.* The first High Elvis's inaugural policy is not politics but
street-sweeping: pick up the rubbish, scrub the footpaths, fix the drains and
the sewers, one hundred thousand people with brooms. *"We will give ourselves
the gift of dignity. It is a potent symbol of defiance."* Later, Silo and Chilo
are sentenced — as penance for what they did to Sarah — to lead the Elvi in
retaking the Travelator carriage by carriage and making public transport safe.
The WIP has the Elvi as a political and musical force and has "Occupy Bent
Street", but nothing where they simply make a place better and are hated for it.
Given Part 6 opens on the inauguration of a *new* High Elvis riffing on the
first, the broom speech is the obvious thing for the new one to echo.

**Sentencing the Sages.** Connected to the above, and separable. In the draft,
Sarah cannot have the Sages executed, imprisoned or tortured — the High Elvis's
cabinet has decided they possess neither the authority nor "the special morality
required to punish anyone" — so the punishment invented is *useful labour at
genuine risk of death.* Sarah's refusal to forgive is intact in the WIP ("I'm
still not letting you off for the evil shit you did to me"), but nothing is ever
demanded of Kleo, Solo or Chilo in return. A penance would give that refusal
somewhere to go.

**The Elvi Parliament as comedy.** *Draft: ch25 "Blessed Mother meets
Parliament", ~L4287–4479.* The WIP has an Elvi Parliament in a former brothel
and a High Elvis with ministers, but the draft plays the institution far harder:
a Sergeant-at-Arms beside himself with excitement, a cabinet arguing about which
chair outranks which for a pregnant visitor and settling it on the grounds that
hers is the only one with a cushion, a Treasurer sacked for leaving the nation's
emergency funds in term deposits ("I thought we would have more time"), a
Minister for External Affairs interrupting a solemn moment with "No, don't
laugh. I've got a gig tomorrow." If Part 6 stages a new inauguration, this is a
ready-made quarry.

**Hermann and Anstey alive.** *Draft: ch27, ~L5473–5573.* The draft's scientists
survive twenty years sealed in a concrete lab because the virus holds their
minds together as well as their bodies, and they walk out through a sewer. The
WIP has closed this off — the corpses are found, "well preserved… hair perfect,"
and the preservation is being used as an oblique hint about the virus, which is
the quieter choice. Flagged only so the decision is a decision: the draft's
version supplies a living expositor for the two viruses at exactly the point
Part 6 needs one, and the WIP will have to find that elsewhere (currently
Kathy).

**Halo's protection cell.** *Draft: ch26 "Crack Protection Team", ~L5259–5313.*
The WIP has Halo as a PAPER agent who protected Chanta for eight years, revealed
by thumb drive. The draft goes further: *all* of Tony's friends were on the
detail — Major, Abe, the "uncle" who raised him and vanished when Tony was
sixteen (tortured to death, and kept his mouth shut, which is why Tony is
alive). Tony has never had a friend in his life and does not know it. He also
owns his apartment and never read the documents that said so. A crueller version
of the same revelation, and the WIP's Major and Abe are currently spare parts
who died in the Stickett Inn. Worth weighing against the risk of over-explaining
a reveal that has already landed.

**The Airborne's money is hostage.** *Draft: ch27 "Anti-virus Virus",
~L5799–5855.* Behind a drape in a restaurant is a door that only opens for
Hardy's blood relatives. Behind it, a dormant control room; behind that, an
apartment; and on a medical table, Jamison Hardy, mummified. Sarah scrapes dust
from his arm into a jar and uses it as the DNA key. What unlocks is not just
Tony's fortune but everyone else's: every wealthy family and most corporations
in the country hid trillions in Hardy's offshore banks and have not been able to
touch the principal since he disappeared. They still receive the dividends. They
cannot withdraw.

The WIP has the inheritance and the DNA verification, but not this. It is an
enormous lever — the Airborne class financially hostage to a cleaner, and the
reason no one could ever find Hardy's heir is that everyone was hunting him.
Whether the ending wants a lever that big is a real question. But the image —
the richest man in history as a pile of dust used as a password — is superb and
costs almost nothing.

**Sarah's dreams identified.** *Draft: ch26, ~L5093–5195.* The pattern burned
into Sarah's nightmares turns out to be a Beta-mode identifier: the pattern of
the gravity waves released at the moment of the Big Bang, *"like looking back in
time, almost back to the beginning of time itself."* The WIP has the nightmares,
the disc's fractal, Little Clinton's neural pattern and the "Standing Infinity
wave" all converging, and the ledger calls this the most load-bearing thread in
the book — but the draft's plain identification, and Sarah's reaction (the
anxiety washing out of her the instant she hears it, replaced by "a warm
contented glow"), is a clean way to let the reader understand without letting
the characters understand.

## 4. Deliberately not recommended

Checked and set aside, with reasons, so the same ground isn't walked twice.

- **The Dunning-Kruger prologue** (ch1). A president nukes his ally's own
  territory to comply with a disarmament treaty, invoices them nine trillion for
  it, and is paid in irradiated gold. Funny in places; but it is a different
  book's opening, it front-loads geopolitics the WIP has replaced wholesale with
  the Layer and the Meritocracy, and it is the draft's most dated material.
  Already present in Project Notes > Summary if wanted.
- **The seven-level class system.** Superseded by Airborne/Cloudborne. No.
- **Kleo's death by exorcism** (ch25, ~L4027–4203). Already excluded by author
  direction and recorded in [[characters/kleo]]. Noting only that the exorcism
  *device* is separable from Kleo's death: heavy water flicked from a vial, the
  power of reason compelling a demon, a possessed Elvis arguing Aquinas and
  Hartle-Hawking, "E=MC2, the power of science compels you." The gospel war in
  the WIP has no comparable scene of the Sages fighting the infection hand to
  hand. If it were ever wanted, it can be had without killing anyone.
- **The Elvi are sterile.** In the draft this is why abortion is unthinkable to
  them and why 100,000 clones would riot. The WIP has replaced it with a
  breeding population descended across centuries, which is better and is already
  load-bearing. Don't reopen.
- **Random Allocation as the route to the marriage.** The draft's device —
  Eloise rigs a supposedly random state marriage lottery so Sarah is allocated to
  Alexander — **is in the WIP, and is the mechanism of the marriage.** In "Eloise
  and Sarah negotiate" Sarah plays the application as a bargaining threat, fills
  the form in on Eloise's own tablet, and the confirmation pings back on that
  same tablet before she can cancel: ALLOCATED PARTNER: Alexander Shakti. Called
  "the Random Allocation trap" in "The Inauguration of the High Elvis." The
  WIP's version is better than the draft's in every respect — Sarah walks into it
  herself, using it as leverage, which is far crueller than being handed a list.
  Nothing to recover.
- **The narrator's misogyny.** Whole passages of the draft are the narration
  itself sneering at women, at gay men, at the poor. The WIP has redistributed
  this into Halo, Jones and Chanta where it belongs. Nothing here should come
  back into the narrator's mouth.

## 5. Quarry — lines, gags and furniture

Small things, no structural cost, take or leave:

- **The Layer's origin** (~L157–161). A product called "air-cleaner" sold as
  "total atmospheric clarification"; it condensed every pollutant ever emitted
  into a permanent layer at two hundred metres; the company explained the process
  had not failed but "worked in an unexpected way" and would require the purchase
  of more product; a dye was added to change it from spew green to silvery white.
  Sales beyond anyone's wildest expectations. The best-constructed joke in the
  draft, and the WIP's Layer has no origin story.
- **Housing** (~L127–131). "Discreet Living Capsules" — sleeping drawers.
  Time-share on your own flat, eight to twelve hours a day, sold to shift
  workers. Gazumpers: people who circulate the dorms trying unlocked drawers,
  hoping for a few unconscious hours at someone else's expense; being gazumped
  means disinfecting the whole drawer. Apartment blocks condemned deliberately to
  maintain the shortage: "People could go ahead and die en-masse, but property
  values could not fall."
- **The public showers** (~L691–695). Cages stacked ten high; clean water enters
  only at the top tier and drains down through the grates; by the bottom rung it
  is cold brown sludge. Price by altitude — the Layer in miniature, indoors.
- **The Big Crush** (~L769–783). See §2.3. Currently a chapter title in Project
  Notes only.
- **Corporate Infidelity** (~L309). A crime of absolute liability: complaining to
  your spouse about work. Guilt and sentence decided by audience vote, carried
  out immediately. "One show, one body, no excuses." The Wheel of Death survived
  into the WIP; the offence that feeds it did not.
- **Pattinson's Mobile Abortions — 1800 BABY-GONE** (~L2179). An ad break in the
  middle of a televised execution.
- **The blank's fee** (~L461). Emil recoils at being recruited as an assassin
  until they explain the target is a paedophile. He is told this because it is
  useful, not because it is true.
- **"He's only been dead 7 hours!"** (~L219). Sarah bids on her dead
  predecessor's apartment before lunch on her first day, and does not think about
  his family — "not that she was totally devoid of human sympathy"; it simply was
  not a thing the company considered important, so no one else did either.
- **The Elvi funeral** (~L4485–4539). Sixty-five thousand acoustic guitars
  joining one at a time from opposite ends of a stadium; then the mourners strip
  off their black to reveal rhinestones. Ninety minutes, then a wake that runs
  for days.
- **Songs.** "Evolve Me Tender", "Burning Coal", "Most Have Never Stirred", "The
  Eighth Concentric Circle of Dante's Hell" are all in the draft in full. Three
  of the four are already parked in the bundle's Unused lyrics folder; "Most Have
  Never Stirred" — the ballad for everyone who was never born — is the best of
  them and would suit a funeral.

## 6. The economists' panel — room, centuries, and how it reaches the reader

### 6.0 What "the panel" is

Hardy assembles a room of **young accountants, economists and finance advisers**
— deliberately people whose names he does not know and refuses to be told ("I
don't know any of your names… You have been brought here precisely because I
don't know you"). Not scientists. The finance department. One question — what
happens to the economy if disease ends — and seven hours to answer it. They come
back with one recommendation.

That is the whole idea, and the reason it is worth having: **the cure is not
killed by a villain. It is killed by a spreadsheet, prepared by juniors, to a
deadline.**

### 6.1 Where the room is: inside the Pale bunker

The virus is in **one** lab, not spread across the program — Gerry's briefing
says so twice ("In one lab, HIBReLabs managed to re-sequence a pararetrovirus";
"Late in their program, Dr Hermann took the same viral platform"). That lab is
the Pale site, reached by tunnelling across from the Stickett Inn bomb site, and
it is the **second-to-last** candidate Gerry opens. Jamison Tower is the
remaining unopened candidate and is not used here.

So the room is **in the facility**, and the reason is forced by the WIP's own
secrecy: the file cannot leave the site, so the modellers come to it. Hardy takes
a dozen juniors down, locks them in an office with a data feed and a catering
order, and waits.

Three things fall out, all better than the draft:

- **The deadline stops being arbitrary.** In the draft, seven hours is how long
  the concrete takes to mix. Underground that is not a coincidence — the
  contractors are already on site, and the panel can hear them.
- **The conclusion convicts its own authors.** "All related research staff must
  be eliminated." By the time they have answered the question they know what is
  in the lab. They qualify. They do not leave the room.
- **It answers a line already on the page.** Gerry: "Dr Hamish Hermann and
  Doctor Anstey, entombed alive. **There were more.**" The "more" now has a face
  — a conference room, a table, and a dozen people in office clothes who were
  never scientists.

### 6.2 Two centuries, not one generation

Hardy to Dunhilton is around **two centuries**. That changes the point of the
item:

- **The suppression held for two hundred years.** Dunhilton's "The economy needs
  an underclass with long, drawn-out illnesses. Horrible ones. Expensive ones."
  stops being his opinion and becomes a two-century-old finding recited as common
  sense by a man who has never heard of the people who found it.
- **The invincibility is the real subject.** Two centuries of TPlus is not good
  health; it is a different order of being, and the planned fade toward the birth
  ([[world-rules/biology]], [[arc-tracker/sarahs-biology]]) is the loss of two
  hundred years of certainty rather than a bad few weeks.

Everything in the draft that depends on one generation is unusable: Hermann
surviving to be rescued (already dead in the WIP — the better call), the
virologist boyfriend being contemporary with anyone now alive, Sarah being
thirty-two, the plague being in living memory.

### 6.3 Nobody finds it. The narrator tells it.

**Author ruling, 2026-08-08.** The narrator tells the reader what happened in
that room. **Only the reader knows.** No character in the book learns it, and
[[characters/agent-jones]] has nothing to do with it. On the page, nobody needs
to know more than what [[characters/sarah]] tells them — that she is very old
and that she carries the virus.

The book's narrator already speaks across universes and directly to the reader:

- "…sang what would be known as Crying in the Chapel **in less decrepit
  universes**" (scene: "Lonnie discovers the jukebox sabotage")
- "that Funeral-Gabber DJ duo that **we** can't name for legal reasons and
  because they threatened to play in **our streets** if we did" (scene: "The
  three little laws")

A narrator who says *our streets* and *less decrepit universes* can say *nobody
ever found out, and nobody ever will* — and is the only one in the book who can.

**Form.** A narratorial aside to the reader. Deadpan. Quote the conclusion and
decline to comment. Five sentences will carry it: who was in the room (juniors,
names deliberately not taken), the question and the seven hours, the sentence
they arrived at, what happened to them, and the fact that it was never known. No
scene, no discovery, nobody present.

**The clause that makes it land** is the last one. Hardy's private consolation
was that he had saved the world and no one would ever know. He was right. The
narrator is the only party in a position to tell us he was right, and telling us
is the joke.

**Placement — the argument for late.** The ending has the restored universe
carrying no memory, record or perceptible seam of having been consumed and
remade ([[review/ending-decisions-log]] §F). The room is the same shape at
domestic scale: an enormous thing that decided everything, that nobody knew and
nobody ever will. Placed near the ending, the juniors are the small rehearsal of
the large joke, and the narrator is telling one story twice. Keep it well away
from the excavation — proximity invites the reader to expect someone to find
out.

**The one rule.** No character shows a flicker of knowing about the panel. Gerry's
"Apparently not" stays a shrug forever; Dunhilton keeps reciting a two-century-old
finding as his own common sense. Only the reader closes that loop. (Sarah's own
history is a separate matter — see §2.2; she knows what happened to her mother
and why.)

### 6.4 What clashes geographically

The WIP, from [[world-rules/geography]] and the two drafted scenes ("Gerry opens
Jamison's labs", "Gerry acquires the bomb site"): HIBReLabs is a dozen-plus
separate installations across Ulyssia and the Pacific territories, each sunk
fifty metres or more under volcanic rock or dense conglomerate and sealed
beneath ten-metre concrete domes; the viral work was in one of them, the Pale
site, less than two blocks from the Stickett Inn bomb site and at the same depth
as its sub-basement.

The draft: one facility; Hardy walks into a security manager's office and orders
"Labs 3 through 10" sealed that afternoon; concrete into "a non-descript hole in
the ground"; the plague starts among the contractors who poured it. Forty metres
of rock, not fifty.

Three clashes: one facility versus a program; Hardy giving the order on site to
a named level-3 security manager; forty metres versus fifty-plus. None costs
anything, because the finding is about a **class of thing**, not about a site —
