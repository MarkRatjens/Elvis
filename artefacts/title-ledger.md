# Chapter Title Ledger — Mondegreen Retitling

Decision record only. Author rulings and approved titles.

**Do not record chapter or scene content here.** Content is dynamic and lives
in the manuscript; summarising it here duplicates the source of truth and goes
stale on redraft. Read the prose at the moment you need it, every time.

A mondegreen is a misheard lyric ... there is no strict requirement for rhyming ... misheard is misheard, regardless

Scope: 33 working-titled chapters need mondegreens — 19 in Part 4, 12 in
Part 5, 7–8 in Part 6 (parked, drafting incomplete).

## Rules (author, 2026-08-03)

1. **Recognisability** — a reader must stand some chance of identifying the
   real song.
2. **Chapter relation is a gradient, not a requirement.** Mondegreens are
   mostly gibberish. Closer to the content is better; no meaning is required
   and none should be manufactured.
3. **Chapter titles derive from SONG TITLES, never lyrics.** Parts are the
   opposite — every part title is a lyric line. Covers count as titles.
   - One override granted: "A Little More Kitchen" (Ch 3) is lyric-derived.
     Exception, not precedent.
4. **Deform the phrase.** Do not twiddle one word. The house style
   re-hears the the title to any extent — *It's Now or Never* → "It's Nose In to Smell a
   Lie"; *Can't Help Falling in Love* → "Cantalope Calling a Bluff".
5. **Suspicious Minds template: the adjective is better as THREE syllables.**
   Four-syllable adjectives don't scan. Proven endings: `-icious`,
   `-itious`, `-ectious`, `-egious`, `-entious`.
6. **Never trust the Scrivener synopses.** They are unmaintained and
   inconsistent — three were wrong in Part 4 alone. Open `content.rtf`.
7. No thematic clustering of chapter source-songs with part source-songs.
   Proposed and rejected.
8. **Chapter isolation.** Evidence for a title should come from that chapter's
   own scene files. Never justify a title with a detail from another chapter,
   and never hold several chapters' content at once — that is how facts bleed
   across boundaries and produce confident, specific, wrong justifications.
   One chapter in, one decision out, then let it go.

## Process

- One chapter at a time. 3–5 candidates, each tagged with its source song.
- Author picks, riffs, or bounces. **No approval by silence** — an undecided
  chapter keeps its working title.
- Candidates generated inline. The `/mondegreen` skill is not used.
- **Check the chapter's own prose for embedded mondegreens first** (ruling
  after "the High Elvis calls a meeting", where "Amazing Grease" was sitting
  in the text while five rounds of candidates missed it). The target is the
  balance between pun and hilarious mishearing — a pun is a valid mondegreen
  when it's plausible as a misheard lyric; neither pure theme-puns nor pure
  sound-gibberish is the house style.
- **Source songs are never "spent"** (author, 2026-08-05). A song may be
  misheard any number of times across the manuscript — Suspicious Minds has
  been riffed a dozen ways, Amazing Grace carries "A Macing Race", "Amazing
  Grease", and "Amazing Grass". Never exclude or flag a candidate because
  its source already appears in the ledger.

## Applying approved titles — RED TIER

Chapter titles live in the `.scrivx`, which Scrivener autosaves over
continuously. Approved titles **queue here** and land in one batch when the
lock clears: back up the `.scrivx`, apply, read back, verify.
