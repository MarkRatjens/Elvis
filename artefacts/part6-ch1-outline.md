# Part 6, Chapter 1 — a working outline

*For drafting. Written 2026-08-13 from decisions the author made 11–13 August.
No numbers anywhere: where the scene wants a quantity, there is a gap marked
`[ ]` for the author to fill.*

---

## Settled, so you don't have to re-decide it

- **Chanta makes the decree**, not the High Elvis, and the chapter ends on it.
- **Nobody is persuaded of anything.** No argument in the room, no one pushing
  back. The chapter has no opposition in it. Refusal belongs to Humble later.
- **The thing is already happening.** Chanta isn't starting anything. He is
  telling people what the thing they are already doing turns out to be for.
- **Kathy has kept nothing from anybody** and knows she is not at fault. She
  comes in with pieces of a puzzle that has not formed. She has never had a
  reason to join them up, because until the sickness there was no question.
- **Why she has never seen this before:** the only Elvi she has examined are
  the dead and the dying, because those are the ones who come to a doctor.
  Nobody turns up at a clinic to report feeling wonderful. Tonight is the
  first time she has been in a room full of the ones who are well.
- **Sarah says one thing all night** and it is not addressed to anyone.
- **Chanta never crosses the room.** Once he sits on the edge of the dais he
  stays there until he stands up to speak. His drinks come to him.
- **Sarah never crosses the room either.** She is in the office chair. The
  room comes past her.
- **People have been exposed at different times**, so what shows on them is at
  different stages — one far gone, another barely started. That unevenness is
  what tells a doctor something is moving through a group rather than a
  coincidence.
- **Nobody remarks that Chanta looks well.** Anybody could see it and nobody
  does. This is never explained or pointed at.

## The shape, in order

### 1 — Before it starts

The pig on the roof with his cold cigar. A party that has been going for hours
before Sarah and the aunts arrive. Kathy wheels in the office chair, kicks the
Holy Momma's throne over and puts the chair in its place. Hayley waves off the
bourbon; Kathy drinks both. Walter takes the Great Teacher's throne with the
signed poster in his fist.

*What this has to do:* put everybody where they will stay, and put the poster
in Walter's hand early enough that it is furniture by the time it matters.

### 2 — Chanta cannot get away

He is circulating, half blinded by his own outfit. What is wrong is not that
he is among people who lost family — it is that everybody has begun looking at
him as though he is about to say something, and he has never said anything on
purpose in his life. He goes to the Elvines he knows on the theory that they
will not want anything from him. The theory fails: they want it too. His
attempt at consolation is a disaster and his offers to catch up later make it
worse.

*Must not slip:* he notices nothing. Not that the Elvines he knows are the ones
still standing, not that anybody looks well. He is oblivious the whole way
through and stays oblivious until the end.

### 3 — Sat down

The Chief of Staff calls places and promises to send a drink over — this is why
he never has to go to the bar again. Walter will not give the throne up, so
Chanta sits on the edge of the dais with the Elvisettes around him, which is as
close to Sarah as he can bear to be.

Kathy makes one remark about the Elvines looking well and lets it go.

*Must not slip:* she cannot ask whether he **knows** them or *knows* them. That
question means she has already joined it up, and she hasn't.

### 4 — The ceremony

Tom Wilson takes the spa bath. Continuity and dignity — he will not deviate, he
is a Clambake through and through, and every High Elvis hereafter will be one
too. He names the two threats: the sickness, and the Holy Rollers' schism. The
song. The mourning passage. The crowd chants the wrong slogan and he takes it
anyway. The baptism fails because nobody checked the plumbing, so they use a
jug of Pepsi.

*What this has to do:* be a full ceremony, warm and slightly shabby, and give
Kathy nothing. She is stuck in a chair for the whole of it, unable to get near
anyone. That is the point of it being long.

### 5 — The room afterwards

It doesn't empty, it churns. A wake and a coronation happening a few metres
apart with nobody sure which they are at. Sarah stays put; people file past to
touch the belly. Kathy goes into the crowd.

*What she is actually doing:* getting sleeves pushed up at a party without
saying why. It is a practical job, not thinking.

### 6 — What she finds

Three people get out of chairs. Two of them do it the way everybody in that
room does it — a hand on the arm of the thing, a pause, a noise. The third just
stands up, still talking. Nobody looks at any of them.

Then the woman with the old tattoo, showing it round, annoyed and delighted at
once. It is old enough that it should be permanent and it is going — the fine
work gone out of the middle, the edges holding on. She is pleased. She wanted
rid of it for years and could not afford it.

Kathy asks about scars. The woman pushes her sleeve up further and finds
nothing there, and her face changes. She was not complaining about that one.

*This is the beat that lands.* Not the tattoo — the scar she had forgotten she
had.

*Gaps to fill:* `[how long she has had the tattoo]`, `[how long since the
person named on it died]`, `[when she noticed it going]`.

*Must not slip:* Kathy does not work anything out here. She recognises
something. She has seen a body behave like this before, in blood she has never
mentioned to anyone, and there is no moment where she says so.

### 7 — Said out loud, near enough for Chanta to hear

Kathy cannot show her working, because it starts with blood she took from
Sarah while Sarah was asleep. So she puts it to Hayley instead and lets Hayley
say the parts she can't.

She reaches for the right word — **salutogenic** — and it lands on nobody, so
she has to say it again in plain language. The plain version is the one Chanta
picks up.

Betty Lee's roots have gone back to their own colour. Marguerita's knee is
better. Hayley asks how long they have been with him and he answers without
understanding why he's being asked.

Sarah, without turning round: *"Then it can be moved."* No weight on it. Not to
anyone.

*Gap to fill:* `[how long Marguerita has been with him]`.

### 8 — He gets there

Not by following the reasoning — he can't. He arrives at the right answer by a
route nobody else can retrace, and he is certain about it in a way that is not
supported by anything he understands. Kathy tries to slow him down. He tells
her she has been saying it wrong.

*Must not slip:* he does not count anything, and he does not become clever. He
is the same man he was two pages ago, who has simply landed on the truth.

### 9 — The decree

He takes the spa bath in the cape and the crown and does not use the
ceremonial voice.

The room goes quiet the way it went quiet once before, for a man on that same
bath who asked them to sweep the streets — and that is the only weight the
moment needs.

What he tells them is **news, not permission**. Nothing is forbidden and
nothing is being allowed. He tells them the thing that saves you is already
here, some of you already have it and don't know, and this is how it travels.
Then he says what it is, and says it again in the plainest word available.

**"Go forth and hump."**

The chapter ends. Nothing after it. No reaction from the High Elvis.

## Still owed, and not by this chapter

**Walter and the poster.** This chapter was created so that somebody sees what
was lost when the first High Elvis died, over Walter's shoulder and without
going inside his head. The signature that is the same one was that moment.
The chapter now ends on the decree, so it has nowhere to sit. It is in the
Holding Block. It needs a home and it gets weaker the further the first High
Elvis recedes.

**Tom Wilson upstaged.** He is crowned on continuity and dignity and is
superseded within minutes by a man with no office at all, and gets no reaction
on the page. Owed to a later chapter — recorded in `characters/the-high-elvis`.
