- **Stock Exchange / Moral Compliance:** Can file injunctions against Cloudborne founders.
- **Real views as scarce tradeable assets:** unlike simulated outlooks (view-projection windows — see [[technology]]), real views from real places "were not bought and sold so much as quietly bid for in some of the most prestigious art auction houses in the world, provided there was not a Banksy tag in sight." Harbour and beach frontage is the scarcest old-money wealth precisely because, unlike sky/altitude, no more of it can be made. (scene: "The Domain")

- **Airborne alienation from the ground:** the Airborne young live above the Layer and are unfamiliar with the ground — they dress (with research and a retained stylist) specifically to blend in on grass, and treat actual grass with astonishment (falling to their knees, stroking it, chewing blades). At the festival they "came down from the towers" to the ground-level Domain. (scenes: "The three laws," "The Sages hijack the stage")
- **Airborne allowance accounts:** Airborne youth are funded by trust-fund "patrons" (parents); goods can be "priced for Airborne allowance accounts only." (scene: "The three laws")
- **Airborne financialisation:** the Airborne recognise exclusivity by price (the [[technology|Air Guitar]] "priced higher, gram-for-gram, than Eastern Suburbs real estate"); one kid registered an air-guitar-derivative on the nation's commodity exchange by close of business, and the Airborne ran books on the festival punch-up outcomes (extends the Stock Exchange / commodity-exchange motif). A national stockpile exists — the "strategic air-guitar reserve" (parody of a strategic petroleum reserve): the only proof the crowd was ever there will be "a song in half a million heads and a dent in the strategic air-guitar reserve." (scenes: "The three laws," "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])
- **Airborne consume Elvi violence as entertainment:** the crowd loved "the double act of concert and fight club"; the ticket read as "a license to participate," and Airborne kids staged their own slow-motion, pulled-karate mock punch-up. (scene: "The three laws")
- **Airborne crowd mimicry (air-guitar culture):** Airborne kids "threw their air guitars in the air... and failed to catch most of them," then, matching the Sages' instrument, "tossed their air guitars and took up air ukuleles instead" — Airborne festival fashion tracks whatever is on stage. Reaffirms the Airborne "playing the markets or running books on the punch-ups." (scene: "The Sages hijack the stage")
- **Class blurs at ground level (festival):** at the Domain festival, telling Airborne from Cloudborne from Elvi is "impossible, which was rather the point." The crowd nonetheless sorts for call-and-response into position ("the back"/"the front") and class ("the Elvi," "the Cloudborne," "the Airborne young"): the **Cloudborne are the numerical majority** ("were legion," "buried everybody"), the genuine Elvi a proud minority, and the Airborne young (down from the towers) are challenged to "outdo the Cloudborne." (scenes: "The three laws," "The Sages hijack the stage")

### Layer System

- The Layer physically separates Airborne (above) from Cloudborne (below). (scene: "[[emil|Emil]] walks into a bar")
- Above: crystalline clarity, night sky, moon, stars visible. (scene: "Emil walks into a bar")
- Below: toxic, bone-eating clouds; oxygen-starved air; atmospheric chemicals condense, causing corrosion. (scene: "Emil walks into a bar")
- The Layer visible from below as "a fluffy blanket" the sun goes behind. (scene: "Tony's health improves, and he goes to buy coffee.")
- The Layer dissolves organic matter before it hits the sidewalk below. (scene: "[[alexander|Alexander]] visits [[hayley|Hayley]]")
- Distance between zones: "only a quick hover-copt jump." (scene: "Sarah & Tony get medical attention")
- The Layer can be displaced by strong winds: "an infrequent westerly conquered the prevailing southeast trade winds, and blew much of the Layer out over the Tasman Sea." Sunny days expose Cloudborne; "The Cloudborne had learned to hate sunny days." (scene: "[[johnny-stoat|Johnny]] tests the discs")
- The Layer cannot dissolve a full-grown elephant carcass. (scene: "Johnny tests the discs")

### Elvi Culture

- Culture centred on karaoke bars in the Pale/Ghetto. "Elvi" preferred plural; "Elvine" for female. (scene: "Sarah celebrates her promotion")
- **Series naming:** Each Elvi is named after the character Elvis played in the film of their series; each Elvine after that film's romantic-interest character. The Elvi never confuse one another despite the shared-origin names — outsiders do, and the confusion reads as the outsider's failing, not theirs. An Airborne matriarch to a pair of Clambake-series Elvi: "you all look the same to me. Doesn't it get confusing?" — "No ma'am. I know exactly who I am." / "And so do I." (scene: "Holy Joint Venture")
- Non-Elvi Elvis impersonation = cultural appropriation; enforced by security. (scene: "Sarah and Tony take refuge in the Memphis Mafia Bar") **At the festival the genuine-vs-impersonator line shows as authentic dance:** the real Elvi are marked by knowing the old dances — "Those that knew how (mostly Elvi) jived, strolled or boogie-woogied, while the rest shook like ecstatic pentecostalists with rubber legs" — and when called in the call-and-response "the Elvi sang louder [than] their numbers might have promised": a proud minority. (scene: "The Sages hijack the stage")
- All Elvi in the Ghetto assumed armed. (scene: "Ajent raids the Memphis Mafia Bar")
- Agree on the music, never the lyrics. (scene: "Vegas show")
- **The lyrics schism (extends "agree on the music, never the lyrics"):** the Elvi divide into those who sing "the Teacher's words" and those who "freelance" (their own lyrics). Singing lyrics not written by the Great Teacher is a point of "lyrical theology" that halts the music and turns ugly — other Elvi charge the stage and assault the performer with karate (e.g. a Tickle Me-series Elvine sang the freelance/mondegreen line "is your arse filled with paint," triggering a mass rush). In the hospitality marquee, Teacher's-words bands ate on the left, freelancers on the right. Elvi quote **mondegreens "as if they were scripture."** Elvi factions "fought all afternoon"; the punch-ups subsided only "after The High Elvis laid down the law" — the [[legal-framework|Three Laws]] decree. **Combat etiquette:** the Elvi hang up their capes before fighting — "there was justifiable violence and then there was dry-cleaning." (scenes: "Vegas show," "The three laws," "Sarah then Eloise arrives," "Chanta's tower")
- **Karate as universal Elvi mode:** Elvi volunteers marshalled, blessed, and directed festival foot traffic with "karate hands" (extends karate-as-Defence). (scene: "The three laws")
- **Rock Against Revival festival organisation (Elvi-run):** the festival operates ticket and bag checks, volunteer marshals, a Chief Security Officer, a musicians' hospitality tent/marquee (with a "rider"; off-stage bands go "straight into a second set in the corner" there), and a VIP platform bearing thrones (site layout in [[geography]]). Cloudborne festival dress: "whatever glittered." A scalper resale economy operates: scalpers resold tickets for "three months' wages" — the High Elvis addresses "those of you who paid a scalper three months' wages for the privilege": they "now know exactly what luck retails for." (scenes: "Sarah then Eloise arrives," "The three laws," "Chanta's tower," "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])
- **Elvisettes:** the female groupie/backstage cohort of the Elvi music scene — distinct from "Elvine" (female clone). **Author-confirmed 2026-08-08: Elvisettes are not clones at all — they are non-Elvi *fans* of the Elvi.** Both terms are canon and neither should be normalised to the other; where a woman in an Elvi setting is a fan rather than a unit, Elvisette is correct. ### Elvi Religion — the Revival schism (Part 5 close)

- **Two opposed factions:** **Evolutionaries** (the Domain festival crowd — worship "the One" / the Dawkins / the accident of existence; the festival **Rock Against Revival** is their rally against the religious Revival) vs **Revivalists / the Holy Rollers** ("the Rollers and their heaven" — the afterlife-believing, gospel sect; Pastor Rex Humble's congregation). (scene: "Religious Minds" [Rock Against Revival])
- **Evolution Elvi (faction; characterised per Chanta's on-page observation):** "making a religion of fighting a religion"; they "wanted to make a profit of the Original Dawkins and were worshipping Little Clinton in ways no kid his age—or even older—should be worshipped." Doctrine as the High Elvis states it — a song "about the Dawkins, about the whole beautiful accident of us, about evolution showing us that everyone here is no better or worse than anyone else. We're all … just here." Religionisation speed (free-indirect Chanta): "turning evolution into a religion took the Elvi about forty-six minutes." Term in use: "**the Original Dawkins**" (variant of the recorded "The Dawkins"). Origin posed as Chanta's on-page suspicion, not asserted fact: "Had he opened the door to religion when he researched the Original Dawkins that day back in the recording studio?" (scene: "Chanta's tower")
- **Evolutionary theology — "the One":** the High Elvis preaches (intimate register, not ceremonial) that existence arose "by the one long blind accident that had been running since before there was a Ghetto," and "the name they had given the accident, when they finally got round to loving it, was the One."
