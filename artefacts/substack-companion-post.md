# Where these figures come from

*Companion post. Link to this from the main article so the argument stays readable and the
evidence stays available. No tables — everything pastes clean.*

---

This is the evidence behind the estimate of how long Elvis Presley might have lived. Every claim is graded, so you can see at a glance which parts are solid and which are only widely repeated.

Two things shape the whole exercise. **The autopsy report has never been released** — the Tennessee Supreme Court ruled in 1982 that it was "the private project of the family," and it has no release date. So every organ finding in circulation describes a document nobody can check, and none of it is used here. What *is* public is the prescribing: found proven by a medical board in 1980 and aired in open court in 1981. That is the spine of the account.

**How each claim is graded**

- **DOCUMENTED** — read at source; multiple independent sources, or a public record.
- **REPORTED** — consistently reported, but resting on description rather than record.
- **INTERESTED** — asserted by a party with a stake in the answer.
- **PROJECTION** — clinical extrapolation. Nobody's finding, including mine.

## DOCUMENTED

**The prescribing**

- Dr George Nichopoulos was his physician from **1967**, first treating him for "saddle pain."
- Prescribing volume: three figures circulate and do not reconcile — over **10,000 doses** in the first eight months of 1977; **19,000 doses** across 1975–77; **12,000 pills and vials** in the final twenty months.
- What was on the lists: **Quaalude, Dilaudid, Amytal, Dexedrine, Valium, Demerol, Carbrital, Placidyl, Percodan.**
- **1980**, Tennessee Board of Medical Examiners: guilty of over-prescription. Three months' suspension, three years' probation.
- **1980** indictment, 14 counts. **Acquitted on all** in 1981.
- Licence permanently revoked — sources give the year as 1993 and as 1995.
- Amphetamines during the Germany posting (arrived **1 October 1958**, discharged **5 March 1960**), introduced by a sergeant on manoeuvres — for energy, "strength" and weight loss.
- In him at death: **Dilaudid, Percodan, Demerol, codeine, Quaaludes.**

**His body, from the public record**

- Anterior uveitis, September 1970 and March 1971.
- Acute angle-closure glaucoma, 1971 — **steroid-induced**, on life-long steroid use.
- **Hypertensive cardiovascular disease and arteriosclerotic heart disease** — the two clinical findings the public record establishes.
- Never became a drinker. Not teetotal.

**The documents**

- The autopsy, **#A77-160**, is private — held so by the **Tennessee Supreme Court, 1982.** Family property, unreleased, no release date.
- Medical Examiner's Report: cause **"H.C.V.D. associated with ASHD"**, manner **natural**, signed by Francisco.
- Francisco's first public statement: **cardiac arrhythmia**, drugs uninvolved, on preliminary findings.
- The Tennessee death certificate becomes public in **August 2027**.

**The family**

- Elvis: born 8 January 1935, died 16 August 1977, **aged 42**.
- Jesse Garon, stillborn twin.
- Gladys: born 25 April 1912, died 14 August 1958, **aged 46** — cause **heart attack**, with liver failure from alcohol a contributing factor, and hepatitis that year.
- Vernon: born 10 April 1916, died 26 June 1979, **aged 63** — **cardiac arrest**, after a heart condition of long standing.
- Lisa Marie: died 12 January 2023, **aged 54** — small bowel obstruction from bariatric adhesions.
- His maternal grandparents were **first cousins**.

## REPORTED

- Codeine at roughly **ten times** therapeutic concentration.
- Obesity in the final years — photographic and universally reported.
- Described as a non-smoker.
- That the twins were identical — could not have been established in 1935.

## INTERESTED

- Nichopoulos's defence: that he was responsible for only **two** of the drugs found in Presley's system.

## PROJECTION

- The life-expectancy estimate itself.

## Not used

- All organ findings attributed to the sealed autopsy — heart weight, colon, emphysema, fatty liver. **Unverifiable.**
