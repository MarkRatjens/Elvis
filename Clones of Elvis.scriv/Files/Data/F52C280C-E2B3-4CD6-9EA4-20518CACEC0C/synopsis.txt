The forward contingent found the road into Sublime Point physically open.

A fence emerged from the bush on either side of it: dark alloy tendrils woven through interlocking triangles and surfaces that seemed to put foliage alternately behind them and in front. There was never any doubt that the fence was there. Looking directly at any particular section merely made its construction harder to settle.

The three gates were down.

Across the openings where the fence stopped, something else occupied the boundary.

It had the dimensions of a barrier without behaving quite like matter. The view through it remained visible, but no longer looked reachable. Light bent across a surface that seemed to possess both depth and skin; textures moved through it without corresponding to anything on either side. The effect was less like looking through glass than looking at somewhere the world had decided not to continue.

Whatever else the display was saying, nobody in the forward party mistook it for a traffic light.

A car approached from inside the compound.

The effect across the outbound lane collapsed. Ordinary road returned. The car passed through. Nothing mechanical moved.

As soon as it was clear, the barrier formed again.

The fence still ended on either side of the road.

The road was still open.

The boundary was not.
