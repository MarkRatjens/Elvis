To the lab in the Pale, Jamison Hardy sent a minion, one Arjen Jansen.
Jansen was led to the lab coat in a room that seemed to be half laboratory, half office and half boarding school lodgings. Only a large window into a brightly lit, less septic, more orderly space suggested any real work was happening. All the lab coat seemed to be doing was glancing at vials, text tubes and spreadsheets and keying notes in to a computer that sat on a  stand-up desk.
Jansen discovered the lab coat’s name was Dr Hamish Hermann, and shortly after would have fallen asleep on his feet, if he weren’t a professional.
“The original TMinusVirus is an engineered pararetrovirus with variable specificity, whereas the derivative TPlusVirus is a chimeric extremophile-augmented pararetrovirus, you see. We spliced a highly advanced genomic payload into the target’s DNA, optimising cellular efficiency. We used tardigrade damage suppressor proteins that shield chromatin against radiation, toxins, and cellular stress; immortal jellyfish transdifferentiation factors for activating continuous cellular rejuvenation that halts biological aging; and vent fungi and anaerobic pathways for enhancing metabolic thresholds for extreme oxygen efficiency and rapid physical tissue healing. We also added a special sauce and some secret herbs and spices.”
Hermann chortled. The minion did not.
“I made a joke,” Hermann said happily. “Are you paying attention?”
“Tardigrades are the little microscopic animals that they tell me look like gummy bears.”
“They are a phylum of segmented micro-animals,” Hermann said flatly. His eyes didn’t leave the computer screen.
“Will it cure cancer?” Jansen asked as if checking off the first a questionnaire.
“Technically, it weaponises it,” Hermann said, his eyes wide with unblinking enthusiasm.
That would have made Jansen sit up and take notice if he wasn’t standing.
“Or rather, it renders it fundamentally obsolete. Cancer is just uncontrolled cellular replication, you see, just cells forgetting how to die. The TPlus platform takes that exact machinery, strips out the malignancy, and recalibrates it using immortal jellyfish transdifferentiation vectors. Instead of forming a tumour, the host’s cells use that hyper-proliferative engine to constantly refresh the epigenetic clock of surrounding tissue. The tardigrade Dsup matrices then act as a genomic shield, preventing the replication errors that cause mutations in the first place. So it doesn’t cure cancer as much as it co-opts the biological framework of it, stabilises it, and turns it into homeostasis. A tumour becomes a tuning fork for biological perfection. You understand, yes?”
“You make it sound like everyone would become immortal.”
Hermann’s chortle made him sound superior. “Oh, no. Do you think me a mad scientist?”
Jansen didn’t answer.
Hermann chewed on the pause, then argued his case. “Complex genomes are fiercely conservative. They hate my beautiful alien code. Because TPlus is a pararetrovirus, its genetic payload doesn’t integrate into the host chromosomes. It remains episomal, floating freely in the nucleus. And what does the cell do to free-floating alien DNA?”
Jansen was stunned to discover Hermann required an answer. “Frankenstein gummy bears?”
Hermann laughed, and it still sounded superior. “It uses histone methylation to pack it away,” Hermann explained. “It silences it. If you give a subject a single dose, they feel like a god for a fortnight.”
The minion frowned. “So it always wears off.”
“Well, yes… and no,” Hermann explained, waving a dismissive hand. “The single dose provides an extraordinary, long-lasting panacea effect, clearing out pre-existing decay, but eventually the epigenetic silencing takes over. The code decays, you see. The cell’s defences will always win. But the beauty of the TPlus platform is its evolutionary design. It is natively engineered for sexual transmission. It spreads horizontally through a population like a common cold, refreshing the host’s cells with every transmission cycle. It bypasses the need for a clinic entirely.”
The minion frowned, his posture stiffening. “It spreads on its own? No needles required after the first wave?”
“Precisely. A self-distributing universal upgrade.”

“Controlled maintenance,” the minion repeated. "What about uncontrolled maintenance? What if someone just takes a massive shortcut—dumps a whole bucket of the stuff into their system?”
Hermann scoffed and waved a hand. “A bucket of my stuff or even a concerted, high-dose regimen would simply accelerate systemic organ failure. The human body does not passively tolerate prolonged genomic intrusion. If you maintain a maximum viral volume over months, the host cells respond by up-regulating their epigenetic defences, aggressively accelerating the methylation rate to crush the virus. Push past that defensive limit with even higher doses, and you trigger a terminal cellular checkpoint. The immune system recognises the persistent, massive foreign load and undergoes catastrophic anaphylaxis. The organs fail completely long before the virus can achieve chromosomal integration.”
“So there’s no risk of it becoming permanent?”
“It’s a statistical absurdity. Permanent integration requires illegitimate recombination—the episomal virus accidentally splicing into a host chromosome," Hermann said. "That mechanical error only occurs if the host cell suffers double-stranded DNA breaks at the exact microsecond its repair enzymes, like ligase IV, are completely saturated by an overwhelming viral volume.  A viral concentration high enough to saturate those repair pathways triggers immediate, systemic toxic shock. The biological engine fails long before the genetic threshold is reached.”
“Can it spread like a regular virus?”
“It can, but an injection is far more effective,” Hermann answered, “and almost as much fun.” Hermann waited for his zinger to land. When it didn’t, he started to say, “I made—”
“Made a joke,” the minion completed. “Yes, I see.”
“Natural horizontal transmission from a carrier is almost as effective as a single injection so, you see, you would still have to consult your family for a  doctor for the bedside manner manages to pass a trace low-titer viral load. A sexual partner will catch it, yes, but they won't get the full panacea effect from it. It’s barely enough to clear a common cold before the host’s methylation systems chew it up and silence it. If they want the actual extended lifespan, the massive cure-all benefits, they still have to come to us for the needle. We force a concentrated volume into the tissue that sex simply cannot replicate."




Hermann let out a short, dismissive bark. "Hardly. We engineered the envelope structure to require a highly concentrated, massive threshold just to breach the cellular membrane. A direct medical injection forces that path easily. But natural horizontal transmission? The viral titer in bodily fluids is entirely insufficient. A sexual partner would receive a trace, sub-threshold exposure easily wiped out by their baseline immune response before the virus ever reached the nucleus. The asset stays contained to the person who paid for the needle."
The minion looked down at the glowing vials on the rack, finally seeing the shape of what Hardy was buying. “So… what exactly are we selling here?"
Hermann smiled, his eyes gleaming with the triumph of a man who had perfectly commodified the human condition. "We are selling a significant life extender that makes you functionally—for want of a better word—invincible. It extends life indefinitely, so long as the client keeps paying for the maintenance threshold. Unless, I guess, you fall from a window."
