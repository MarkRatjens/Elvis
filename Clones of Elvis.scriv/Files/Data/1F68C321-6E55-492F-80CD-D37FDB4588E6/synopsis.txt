Gates open on the Domain and the crowd swamps the merch tents -- glittering T-shirts, capes, ponchos, Elvi-inspired wigs, stick-on sideburns, and collectible Rock Against Revival "Air Guitars" -- soft cases holding nothing but air, just enough to approximate the volume of an electric guitar, until the case's UNLOCK button releases it through a series of pipes tuned, collectively, to a drop D power chord. A lot of money for nearly nothing, and the queue for them is the longest in the field. Bar tents do brisk trade in elaborate Elvi-inspired cocktails that are barely alcoholic but punishingly sweet and fatty for the Airborne crowd; by mid-afternoon there's puke ringing the fringes as the heat rises. Onstage, bands escalate across the day -- three-pieces at the start, growing larger set by set -- but some of the earlier acts sing lyrics not written by the Great Teacher, which sets off the day's running gag: other Elvi charging the stage to assault the offending performers, plus parallel scraps in the green room. The crowd -- roughly half a million strong on the lawn, evenly split Cloudborne and Airborne youth with the Elvi a minority among them -- loves the music and the punch-ups in equal measure; the Airborne kids start up their own mock, good-natured punch-up at the margins. The High Elvis steps in -- dead calm, counselling register, no bombast -- and lays down three laws: sing the words of the Great Teacher; do not punch up on anyone who does not sing the words of the Great Teacher; sing the words of the Great Teacher.
