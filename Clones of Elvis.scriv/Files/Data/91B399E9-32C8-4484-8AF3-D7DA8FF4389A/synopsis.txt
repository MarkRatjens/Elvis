2. The quake-trance diversion
This is the one place where the narrator can pull well away from Hayley, but it still needs to behave like story, not encyclopaedia.
The spine remains the Box.
Something happens in a warehouse. People respond to it. The consequences drive the parties into the desert. The desert changes how the DJ uses the Box. The experiences generate stories. Stories generate gods. Gods generate ritual. Ritual generates the Order. Commercial imitation pushes the Order elsewhere. Goa changes the physical behaviour. Then the joke becomes dangerous.
That gives every piece of exposition a cause for the next piece to exist.
The humour should grow inside that chain:
venues getting condemned is why they leave warehouses;
the desert is where the Box becomes something more than freakishly effective bass equipment;
burnt-sofa directions belong to the growing desert scene rather than appearing because we have a joke to deploy;
Ludwig, Zildjian and Rickenbacker emerge because ravers keep experiencing particular classes of phenomena;
commercial quake-trance is funny because it is attempting to sell something it cannot actually reproduce;
Goa is funny because the Order thinks it has escaped corruption into virgin territory while unknowingly taking trance home;
Wet Quake-Trance becomes alarming because it works much too well.
The narrator can digress, but every digression should have the sense that one absurd fact has forced us to consider another absurd fact, rather than that the author has opened another drawer of prepared jokes.
