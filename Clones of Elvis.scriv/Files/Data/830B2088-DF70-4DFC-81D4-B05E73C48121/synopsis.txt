**Locations**: Memphis Mafia Bar & Grill; surrounding early-morning silence within the Ghetto.

**Tone and Voice Sentence**: "His whole life was storms and strife."

**Synopsis**:
In the pre-dawn hours, Agent Ajent Jones leads a covert mission to infiltrate the Memphis Mafia Bar & Grill, executing his part of Gerry Hardman’s plan to incite discord among the Elvi by replacing their beloved jukebox songs with gospel records. Driven by meticulous spite, Jones meticulously swaps out vinyl discs on a practiced Seeburg LPC-1 Select-O-Matic, sneering at the titles and contemplating the humiliation these records would bring to the Elvi community.

Testing the records, however, Jones inadvertently subjects himself to their emotional pull. Though initially bemused and irritated by the gospel songs, their themes of redemption, hope, and equality begin to penetrate his hardened demeanor. Overcome by conflicting emotions, he cycles through more records, increasingly affected by the melodies and messages until they uncover the fragile, neglected humanity buried under his layers of violence and loathing. A poignant moment arrives as one song’s lyrics resonate deeply with his unspoken anguish, driving him to unexpected tears. Reduced to sitting against the jukebox, heavy with newfound vulnerability, Jones weeps openly—awash in the weight of his own long-suppressed despair, his mission and objectives temporarily forgotten.
