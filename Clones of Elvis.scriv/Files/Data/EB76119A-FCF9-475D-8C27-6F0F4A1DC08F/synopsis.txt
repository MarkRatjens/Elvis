
- The major is invited to a residents’ meeting, given three minutes to explain the incursion, then ruled out of order for discussing military matters.
- Barricades admit residents, cleaners, gardeners and wine deliveries. The distinction between essential workers and government employees is settled by who employs them.
- A heritage application is lodged for the street’s uninterrupted absence of armed government personnel.

- A retired judge serves the major with an injunction over breakfast. His wife asks whether the troops could keep their wounded off the verge until the photographer has been.
- Private militia occupy the road under the auspices of a neighbourhood picnic. Their machine guns are listed as outdoor furniture.
- Residents insist that their own armed patrols constitute neighbourhood character. The army’s constitute militarisation.
- An army vehicle damages a dry-stone wall. Its repair becomes the first demand in every subsequent negotiation, including negotiations about casualties.
- The soldiers are offered accommodation at ruinous holiday rates. Hosts then report them for conducting business from residential premises.
