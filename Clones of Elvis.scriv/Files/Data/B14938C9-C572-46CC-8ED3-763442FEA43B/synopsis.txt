Late afternoon tipping into evening. While Sarah holds the platform (scene 2: "the platform, and Eloise arrives"), Chanta spends the day as already established there -- carousing with bands and Elvisettes in the green room, his throne on the platform conspicuously empty, unable to face Sarah. As the High Elvis's closing set nears, the High Elvis corners him in the green room, dead calm: "there would have been less fighting if there were more lyrics." He needs just one song -- about the Dawkins, about evolution -- to finish the set on and help turn the crowd against the Holy Rollers. Chanta has nothing to give him.

Despondent over Sarah and Little Clinton all day, Chanta climbs the engineering tower that stands behind the VIP platform, believing -- wrongly, per his own long-running private theory -- that proximity to Sarah and the baby will unlock him. Alone at the top, what he actually turns over is rage: the Holy Rollers gutting the Memphis Mafia Bar, wrecking the Ghetto. The setting sun over the lawn triggers his recurring vision -- Little Clinton as an orange ball of light curled in Sarah's belly. Half psychotropic, half sugar-drunk on the festival's cocktails, he tips into an out-of-body flight: he leaves his body slumped on the tower, scribbling, and "arrives," purely in vision, to occupy his empty throne beside Sarah. Chanta never goes on.

From that vantage he sees past the Holy Rollers to the Evolution Elvi themselves -- kneeling to Sarah and Little Clinton as "The One After the One," every bit as credulous as the sect he came up here to write against. The song widens: not one sect, but religious minds as such. Lyrics arrive faster than he can hold them. He grabs a notepad from beside the tower's sound desk and scribbles, texts the High Elvis -- "I have a song" -- and rushes back down, not to the stage, not to the platform, but to the green room.
