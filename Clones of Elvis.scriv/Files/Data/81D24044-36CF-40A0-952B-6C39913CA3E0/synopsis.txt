What is genuinely valuable about The Sages’ Night Out

Its strongest potential function is that it is one of the rare opportunities to see the three Sages alone together.

Everywhere else they are usually operating in somebody else’s story: Sarah’s prophecy, Johnny’s experiment, Chanta’s irritation, the Elvi’s religion. Alone, they can stop functioning as a three-headed oracle and become three people who do not necessarily agree about what their own prophecy means.

That is worth having because the later Project Kebab material already wants a division:

Chilo and Kleo push. Solo cautions.

Without some earlier private fracture, that can look like convenient endgame behaviour. A Night Out scene can establish that the disagreement has been there for a while.

Importantly, I would not make the disagreement:

Chilo wants to destroy the universe.
Solo and Kleo want to save it.

That is exactly the black-hat/white-hat problem you noticed.

The much richer disagreement is something closer to:

They all believe they are fulfilling the same prophecy, but they disagree about what the increasingly strange Gyges-disc results mean and therefore how far they are entitled to push them.

Chilo can be the most confident interpreter. Kleo can think empirical success justifies proceeding. Solo can increasingly suspect they are treating an observation, message or warning as an instruction.

Nobody needs to know that Sarah's waters breaking will end the universe.

Indeed, they probably should not.

What is badly wrong with the existing draft

The rough draft spends virtually all of the scene's potential ambiguity.

Chilo explicitly says:

Project Kebab is proceeding as foreseen;
Johnny is being manipulated;
the dimensional barriers are thinning;
the old order must dissolve;
the universe is fundamentally flawed;
the Dawkins will establish new physical laws;
free will should disappear;
Sarah's waters breaking will coincide with maximum resonance;
the child positively wants this.

That doesn't plant the ending. It explains the ending in advance.

It also turns Chilo into a conventional apocalyptic villain and gives Solo and Kleo the conventional job of saying, essentially, But think of all the innocent people. The peculiar uncertainty of the Sages disappears.

There is also obsolete material in it. The draft talks about midnight on the solstice, which no longer describes your Christmas-morning climax, and its Chilo-versus-Solo-and-Kleo alignment conflicts with the more recent Kebab progression in which Chilo/Kleo push and Solo cautions.

So none of that should constrain the new timeline.

The wrestling idea

Oddly, I think the wrestling may have more salvage value than the cosmology.

Three ancient-looking intellectual frauds/prophets having a serious disagreement and eventually settling some portion of it through an archaic physical contest is quite compatible with the book.

But the current draft overdetermines the joke:

Greek-themed bar
→ Greek food
→ Greek vessels
→ Greek quotations
→ fake ancient music
→ Greek professors
→ shirts come off
→ pankration
→ spectators think it's entertainment.

Everything points at the same joke.

You could retain the essential absurdity—the Sages' idea of an argument eventually becomes physical in some antiquated, highly rule-bound fashion—without making the entire room dress up as Ancient Greece to announce it.

And the fight should not decide who is morally right. It might decide something ridiculously narrower: who gets to set a parameter, whether another test proceeds, who talks to Johnny, or who has to tell Sarah.

That would be much more useful.
