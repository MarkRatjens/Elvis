2. UDF attempt to disarm Shakti forces — 19:12–19:17
	•	Once inside, the UDF begins taking control. The demand to surrender weapons reveals what its assistance entails.
	•	The first private guards treat the demand as a mistake. They explain who they are, whose premises these are, or whose side they are on.
	•	The demand persists. Recognition turns to outrage: they have admitted another force that intends to subordinate them.
	•	A brief comic incident gives that outrage its particular flavour—professional insult, attachment to an expensive rifle, suspicion that an underfunded soldier simply wants better equipment.
	•	Someone tries to bring the automated defences back into the dispute. The access concession now matters: the UDF is already inside, close to the people and controls needed to reverse it.
	•	The disarming attempt produces uneven results. Some personnel surrender weapons; others resist, withdraw or seek to restore their advantage. The disagreement becomes fighting.
	•	End on one useful consequence for the main narrative: a route contested, a position abandoned, access restricted, or guards diverted from the people they were pursuing. The wider struggle can continue beyond the passage.
