As the High Elvis prepares to take the stage for his own set, the three active Sages -- Kleo, Chilo, Solo -- climb up unannounced, ukuleles in hand. Kleo tells him, "Just a little public service announcement," and the High Elvis, in his established calm and counselling register, waves them through without fuss. They play a ditty once through, verbatim: "Praise the Dawkins / He's the One / Praise the Dawkins / And the One after the One / The Holy Momma / And her unborn Son / Praise the Dawkins / To Kingdom Come." Then they turn it into a call-and-response game, working the half-million-strong crowd section by section -- the back, then the front; then just the Elvi; then the Cloudborne, marvelling at the volume ("wow, that was loud"); then a challenge to the Airborne kids to outdo the Cloudborne kids. Kleo takes a ukulele lead break "full of hammers and pulls that would make a Los Angeles Guitar Academy graduate proud" -- technically dazzling, tonally baffling, leaving the crowd wondering why stringed instruments were ever invented. On the platform, the Holy Momma (Sarah), Aunt Kathy and Aunt Hayley react in shock; Eloise laughs harder than she has in decades: "Oh sweet Holy spreadsheets, these guys are good for business." The Sages get the prophecy's liturgy into half a million mouths as a competitive singalong, belief entirely optional -- then the moment passes and the concert moves on toward the High Elvis's own set. This is the Sages' entire presence at Rock Against Revival.
