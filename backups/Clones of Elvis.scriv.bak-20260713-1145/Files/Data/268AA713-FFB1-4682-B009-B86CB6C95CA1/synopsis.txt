The High Elvis opens his closing set addressing the crowd, the content echoing his inauguration's themes -- the One, the One After the One, the Holy Momma delivering Him -- delivered in his established calm, counselling register. He recites the safe, copyright-cleared ballad "Most Have Never Stirred," a Dawkins tribute on the lottery of existence. Later in the set, ahead of the final song, he announces great news: the Great Teacher has written new material, never performed before -- a song called "Religious Minds." The song runs as a through-line, the narration cutting on its turns to three listeners in sequence: Sarah, who hears an accusation she can't answer as Little Clinton stirs and her hand betrays her at her belly; Eloise, who hears a product -- "Number one by Tuesday... Every market. Tax it."; and, across the Layer in the Ghetto, Pastor Rex Humble, who hears it rise through the floor of the walk-in cooler that used to be the Memphis Mafia Bar, some of his own flock already mouthing the words. The crowd sings along because it's a genuinely good song at a concert, not because anything has been installed in them. Nothing detonates on the lawn. The set -- and the scene -- closes on its own weightless terms.
