**Locations**: Former Clambake brothel; now the Elvi Parliament House in the Ghetto.

**Tone and Voice Sentence**: "With a single act, we can show the world we are no longer jokes, no longer just clones of a long-dead entertainer; we are serious."

**Synopsis**:
In the re-purposed luxury of the Clambake, now Parliament House for the Elvi, The High Elvis addresses a crowd of fervent followers during the formal inauguration of The High Elvis. Surrounded by a vibrant mix of second-hand glamour and sequinned chaos, he delivers a rousing speech infused with Elvis song references, urging unity, self-determination, and a reclamation of dignity. Declaring a new direction for the Elvi community, he proposes a bold and symbolic act: to clean up the Ghetto, both literally and figuratively, as a demonstration of their strength and humanity.

While his speech electrifies the crowd, earning chants of “Praise the Dawkins” and rapturous adoration, his final proposition is met with a stunned silence. The Elvi, envisioning grander ambitions as artists and influencers, recoil at the suggestion of manual labor. The tension underscores the challenge of balancing their lofty self-perception with grounded, community-minded action.
