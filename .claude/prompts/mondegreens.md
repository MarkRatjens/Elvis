## **Layered Rules and Process**

### **1. Primary Rule – “Suspicious Minds” Mondegreens (Now Dual-Ending)**
- All titles must be plausible mondegreens (in rhyme, syllable count, and phonetic feel) of “Suspicious Minds.”
    - **Accepted endings:**  
      - Classic: ***-inds, -ines, -igns, -ines, -ines, -ines, -finds, -lines, -signs, -pines, -vines, -fines, -wines, -mines, etc.***  
      - Now also: ***final “-i” sound: -ie, -y, -ai, -igh, -i, etc.*** (e.g. *Tie, Sigh, By, Fly, Guy, Fungi, Pie*).
- Never force awkward, unidiomatic phrases—keep both sound and sense plausible.

### **2. Scene-Specific Thematic Adaptation**
- Every title must attach directly to key scene themes, action, or comic flavour.
    - (“Fungi Sigh” for comic mishap; “Dubious Signs” for paranoia/missing clues; “Ridiculous Guy” for social farce.)

### **3. Exception Rule – Wider Elvis Canon**
- Only offer mondegreens from outside “Suspicious Minds” when **explicitly authorised** (or for specific Tony & Sarah double-play scenes, as previously negotiated).

### **4. Mandatory Variation**
- Each set must offer **at least 12 diverse titles**, not merely variants of “Minds.”
- Strongly encourage blending both:  
    - Classic endings (“-inds,” “-ines,” “-igns,” etc.)  
    - And new “-i” endings (“Tie,” “Fungi,” “Sky,” “Sigh,” “Pie,” etc.)
- Prioritise thematic connection, comic tone, and idiomatic plausibility for all.

### **5. Thematic Notes**
- Every title gets a brief parenthetical note on scene/character/comic fit.
